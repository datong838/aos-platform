# Advertising & Marketing

A technique skill for cross-border e-commerce advertising and marketing. This
skill guides AI agents through paid traffic acquisition: from validating ad
readiness, through PPC campaign setup, to social media advertising and influencer
partnerships. Every recommendation must be grounded in measurable ROI, never in
vanity metrics.

---

## Core Principle

**Advertising amplifies what already works. A bad listing with a big ad budget
just burns money faster. Fix the listing first, then scale with ads.**

A product with poor images, no reviews, and an uncompetitive price will convert
poorly no matter how much traffic you send to it. Every dollar spent on ads for
an unready listing is a dollar wasted. The first question in any advertising
conversation is not "how much should I spend?" but "is the listing ready to
convert paid traffic?"

If a seller asks "how do I get more traffic?" and their listing has fewer than
7 images, fewer than 15 reviews, or a conversion rate below the category average,
redirect them to fix the listing before touching ad spend.

---

## Ad Readiness Checklist

Before spending any money on advertising, the listing must pass ALL of these
prerequisites. A single failure means the listing needs work before ads begin.

| # | Checkpoint | Minimum Threshold | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Main image quality** | Professional white-background photo, compliant with platform standards | Main image is the ad creative for Sponsored Products; poor image = poor CTR = wasted spend |
| 2 | **Image count** | 7+ images including lifestyle, infographic, and size reference | More images = higher conversion rate; ads drive traffic, images close the sale |
| 3 | **Review count** | 15+ reviews with 3.8+ star average | Products with <15 reviews convert at roughly half the rate of products with 50+; ad spend at low review counts has poor ROI |
| 4 | **Price competitiveness** | Within 15% of top-5 competitor average (or justified premium with clear differentiation) | Shoppers compare prices; if yours is notably higher without visible justification, traffic bounces |
| 5 | **Conversion rate** | At or above category average (check Business Reports / analytics) | If organic traffic already converts poorly, paid traffic will convert worse; fix the listing first |
| 6 | **Stock availability** | 30+ days of inventory at projected ad-boosted sales velocity | Running out of stock while ads are live wastes the ranking momentum ads build; budget is lost |
| 7 | **Listing content** | Complete title, 5 bullet points, A+ Content / Brand Story (if eligible) | Incomplete listings signal low trust; conversion suffers on every traffic source |
| 8 | **Buy Box ownership** | Seller holds the Buy Box >90% of the time | Ads that fire when you do not own the Buy Box send traffic to a competitor's offer |

**Decision rule:** If 6 or fewer checkpoints pass, do not start ads. Fix the
gaps first (see `cbec-ops:product-listing`). If 7 pass, proceed cautiously with
a limited test budget. If all 8 pass, proceed with full campaign setup.

## The Traffic Acquisition Pyramid

Traffic sources are layered by cost efficiency. Always maximize the cheaper layer
before scaling to the next.

```
                    /\
                   /  \
                  / EXT \        External (blogs, PR, email)
                 /-------\       Highest cost per click, hardest to track
                /INFLUENCER\     Influencer / KOL partnerships
               /------------\    Medium-high cost, brand halo effect
              / SOCIAL MEDIA  \  Social ads (Meta, TikTok, Pinterest, Google)
             /----------------\  Medium cost, audience building
            /    PPC (SEARCH)   \ Platform PPC (Amazon SP, Walmart SA)
           /--------------------\ Moderate cost, high intent
          /      ORGANIC          \ Organic search, SEO, listing optimization
         /________________________\ Lowest cost, highest ROI, slowest to build
```

**Layer rules:**

1. **Organic first.** Optimize listing, collect reviews, improve keyword indexing.
   Organic traffic is free and compounds over time.
2. **PPC second.** Platform search ads capture high-intent buyers actively
   searching for your product. Start here for paid traffic.
3. **Social media third.** Social ads build awareness and drive consideration.
   Use after PPC is profitable and you want to scale beyond search demand.
4. **Influencer fourth.** Influencer partnerships create social proof and content
   assets. Use when you have a product story worth telling and need credibility.
5. **External last.** PR, blogs, email campaigns, and off-platform SEO. Highest
   effort, longest payback, but builds durable brand equity.

Never jump to a higher layer while a lower layer is underperforming. Fix the
foundation first.

## Campaign Budget Allocation Framework

### New Product Launch (First 90 Days)

Total monthly ad budget should be 25-35% of projected monthly revenue during
launch. This is an investment phase; profitability comes after ranking is
established.

| Channel | Budget Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Platform PPC (auto campaigns) | 30% | Keyword discovery, indexing, initial data |
| Platform PPC (manual exact) | 25% | Convert on validated high-intent keywords |
| Platform PPC (manual broad/phrase) | 15% | Expand keyword coverage at controlled cost |
| Sponsored Brands / Display | 10% | Brand visibility and category presence |
| Social media ads | 10% | External traffic bonus (Amazon rewards this with ranking boost) |
| Influencer seeding | 10% | Content creation and social proof |

### Established Product (Post-Launch, Steady State)

Total monthly ad budget should be 10-18% of monthly revenue. Focus shifts from
discovery to efficiency.

| Channel | Budget Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Platform PPC (manual exact) | 40% | Profitable, high-converting keywords |
| Platform PPC (manual broad/phrase) | 15% | Ongoing keyword expansion |
| Sponsored Brands / Display | 15% | Defensive brand positioning and retargeting |
| Social media ads | 20% | Scale beyond search demand ceiling |
| Influencer partnerships | 10% | Ongoing content refresh and social proof |

### Scaling Phase (Proven Product, Expanding)

Total monthly ad budget should be 15-22% of monthly revenue. The product has
proven unit economics; now scale volume.

| Channel | Budget Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Platform PPC (all types) | 35% | Maintain search dominance |
| Social media ads | 30% | Aggressively acquire new audiences |
| Influencer partnerships | 15% | Larger campaigns with mid-tier creators |
| Sponsored Brands Video / DSP | 10% | Upper-funnel awareness |
| External / PR / email | 10% | Brand building and direct customer relationships |

## ACOS / ROAS / TACOS Target Setting

### Key Formulas

```
ACOS  = Ad Spend / Ad Revenue x 100
        (Advertising Cost of Sales; lower is better)

ROAS  = Ad Revenue / Ad Spend
        (Return on Ad Spend; higher is better)

TACOS = Total Ad Spend / Total Revenue x 100
        (Total Advertising Cost of Sales; measures ad efficiency against ALL sales)

Break-even ACOS = Pre-Ad Net Margin %
        (The ACOS at which you make zero profit on ad-driven sales)

Target ACOS = Break-even ACOS - Desired Profit Margin %
        (The ACOS that preserves your profit target)
```

### Worked Example

```
Selling Price:           $29.99
COGS + Freight + Duties: $8.50
Platform Fees:           $7.50  (referral + fulfillment)
Returns Reserve (5%):    $1.50
Other Costs:             $1.00

Pre-Ad Net Margin:       $29.99 - $18.50 = $11.49
Pre-Ad Net Margin %:     $11.49 / $29.99 = 38.3%

Break-even ACOS:         38.3%  (above this, you lose money on every ad sale)
Target ACOS (15% profit): 38.3% - 15% = 23.3%
Equivalent Target ROAS:  1 / 0.233 = 4.29x
```

### ACOS Benchmarks by Stage

| Product Stage | Target ACOS | Target TACOS | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch (weeks 1-4) | 40-60% (above break-even is acceptable) | 25-35% | Buying ranking and reviews; expect losses |
| Growth (months 2-3) | 25-35% | 15-25% | Tightening toward profitability |
| Steady state (month 4+) | 15-25% | 8-15% | Must be profitable; organic sales should be growing |
| Mature / optimized | 10-20% | 5-12% | Ad spend maintains position; organic does heavy lifting |

### TACOS as the North Star

ACOS measures only ad-attributed sales. TACOS measures ad spend against ALL
sales, including organic. A rising ACOS is acceptable if TACOS is flat or
declining, because it means ads are generating organic sales momentum (the
"halo effect").

**Decision rules:**
- TACOS declining month-over-month: ads are working. Maintain or cautiously increase spend.
- TACOS flat: ads are sustaining position. Optimize for efficiency.
- TACOS rising month-over-month for 3+ weeks: ads are becoming less efficient. Diagnose the cause (increased competition, listing quality drop, seasonality shift, or bid inflation).

## Campaign Optimization Cycle

### Weekly Routine (30-60 minutes per product)

**Monday: Performance Review**
1. Pull the last 7 days of campaign data (allow 48-72 hours of attribution lag)
2. Calculate ACOS, ROAS, and TACOS for each campaign
3. Compare against targets set in the framework above
4. Flag any campaign with ACOS more than 50% above target

**Tuesday: Keyword Management**
1. Review search term report from auto campaigns
2. Identify converting search terms (3+ orders, ACOS below break-even)
3. Promote winning terms to manual exact-match campaigns
4. Add non-converting high-spend terms as negative exact keywords
5. Add irrelevant search terms as negative phrase keywords

**Wednesday: Bid Optimization**
1. For keywords with ACOS below target and good volume: increase bid 10-15%
2. For keywords with ACOS above target but converting: decrease bid 10-15%
3. For keywords with high spend and zero conversions (7+ clicks): pause or
   reduce bid by 30%
4. For keywords with good ACOS but low impressions: increase bid 20-25%

**Thursday: Budget Reallocation**
1. Shift budget from underperforming campaigns to outperformers
2. Ensure no campaign is limited by daily budget (check "budget exceeded" alerts)
3. Review placement performance (top of search vs rest of search vs product pages)
4. Adjust placement bid multipliers based on conversion data

**Friday: Creative and Listing Check**
1. Review ad creative performance (CTR by headline, image, video)
2. Check if listing changes impacted conversion rate
3. Review competitor ad activity (new sponsored brands, video ads)
4. Plan next week's tests (new keywords, bid strategies, creatives)

### Monthly Review (2-3 hours per product)

1. Full TACOS trend analysis (is overall efficiency improving?)
2. Keyword portfolio review (which keywords drive profit vs. burn cash?)
3. New keyword research round (search term expansion, seasonal terms)
4. Campaign structure audit (consolidate or restructure as needed)
5. Budget reforecast based on trailing 30-day performance
6. Competitor advertising analysis (who is outbidding you and where?)

## Red Flags and Common Mistakes

1. **Running ads on a listing that fails the Ad Readiness Checklist.**
   This is the single most common advertising mistake. Sellers start ads on
   a listing with 3 images, 2 reviews, and no A+ Content, then conclude "PPC
   doesn't work for my product." PPC worked fine; the listing couldn't convert
   the traffic. Always complete the readiness checklist before spending.

2. **Setting bids based on "suggested bid" without calculating break-even ACOS.**
   Platform-suggested bids optimize for the platform's revenue, not yours. Always
   calculate your break-even ACOS first, then derive your maximum CPC:
   `Max CPC = Average Order Value x Conversion Rate x Target ACOS%`

3. **Never harvesting keywords from auto campaigns.**
   Auto campaigns are keyword research tools, not set-and-forget profit centers.
   If you are not reviewing search term reports weekly and promoting winners to
   manual campaigns, you are leaving performance on the table and paying premium
   CPCs for broad matching.

4. **Spending on social ads before PPC is profitable.**
   Social ads drive awareness traffic with lower purchase intent. If your high-
   intent PPC traffic is not converting profitably, lower-intent social traffic
   will perform worse. Fix PPC first.

5. **Evaluating influencer ROI by direct sales only.**
   Influencer marketing generates brand searches, social proof, content assets,
   and listing conversion lift that are not captured by last-click attribution.
   Track branded search volume, direct traffic increases, and listing conversion
   rate changes alongside direct sales.

6. **Scaling ad spend linearly without checking marginal ACOS.**
   The first $500/month of ad spend is almost always more efficient than the
   next $500. As you scale, marginal ACOS increases because you exhaust the
   highest-intent keywords first. Scale in increments of 15-20% and measure
   marginal efficiency at each step.

7. **Ignoring the TACOS trend while obsessing over campaign-level ACOS.**
   A campaign with 35% ACOS that drives significant organic ranking lift
   (visible as declining TACOS) is more valuable than a campaign with 15%
   ACOS that generates no organic momentum. Always monitor TACOS as the
   primary health metric.

8. **Running the same ad creative for months without testing.**
   Creative fatigue is real, especially on social platforms. CTR declines over
   time as the same audience sees the same ad repeatedly. Rotate creatives
   every 2-4 weeks and test at least 2-3 variations at all times.
