# Brand Building

Build defensible brand equity on cross-border e-commerce platforms. This skill
covers brand registration, IP protection, storefront design, and brand
storytelling -- everything required to move from commodity seller to recognized
brand.

## Core Principle

> A brand is a moat. Commodity sellers compete on price. Brand sellers compete
> on trust. Invest in brand when you have product-market fit confirmed with at
> least 2 validated SKUs generating consistent sales for 3+ months.

Branding is not a logo. It is the accumulated trust, recognition, and perceived
value that lets you charge a premium, defend against hijackers, and survive
price wars. Every decision below flows from this principle.

**Key decision factors:**

   creation, storefront design). Budget 3-5% of revenue for brand investment.
4. **Portfolio trajectory** -- Single-SKU brands are fragile. Plan for at
   least 3-5 SKUs under the brand umbrella.

## Brand Registry Workflow

Each platform has its own brand registration program with different
requirements and benefits. Follow the platform-specific steps below.

### Amazon Brand Registry

1. **Prerequisite:** Active or pending trademark in the country of the
   marketplace (word mark or design mark with text).
2. **Application:** Enroll at `brandservices.amazon.com`. Provide trademark
   registration number, product categories, countries of manufacture.
3. **Verification:** Amazon sends a code to the trademark office contact.
   Respond within 30 days.
4. **Timeline:** 2-4 weeks after trademark verification.
5. **Benefits unlocked:** A+ Content, Brand Analytics, Brand Store, Vine,
   Transparency, Project Zero, Sponsored Brands ads.

### Shopee Mall

1. **Prerequisite:** Valid business license, trademark certificate, brand
   authorization letter (if not trademark owner).
2. **Application:** Apply through Shopee Seller Centre > Shopee Mall.
3. **Verification:** Document review (5-10 business days).
4. **Benefits unlocked:** Mall badge, priority search ranking, higher buyer
   trust signals, access to Mall-exclusive campaigns.

### TikTok Shop Brand Program

1. **Prerequisite:** Trademark registration, business license, brand
   authorization.
2. **Application:** Apply via TikTok Shop Seller Center > Brand Program.
3. **Verification:** 3-7 business days.
4. **Benefits unlocked:** Brand badge, brand tab in shop, priority in brand
   campaigns, branded content tools.

### Lazada LazMall

1. **Prerequisite:** Trademark certificate, business registration, brand
   authorization letter.
2. **Application:** Apply through Lazada Seller Center > LazMall.
3. **Verification:** 5-15 business days.
4. **Benefits unlocked:** LazMall badge, 15-day return policy handling,
   priority customer support, campaign access.

### Walmart Brand Portal

1. **Prerequisite:** USPTO trademark registration (for US marketplace).
2. **Application:** Apply via Walmart Brand Portal.
3. **Verification:** 2-4 weeks.
4. **Benefits unlocked:** Brand shelf, enhanced content, IP protection tools.

> For full details on each platform, see
> `references/brand-registry.md`.

## Trademark Filing Strategy

### Which Markets First

File trademarks in this priority order:

1. **Primary sales market** (where you sell the most).
2. **Manufacturing country** (usually China -- prevents domestic copycats
   from registering first).
3. **Expansion markets** (file 6-12 months before planned launch).

### Filing Route

| Route | Best For | Timeline | Cost (approx.) |
|-------|----------|----------|----------------|
| Madrid Protocol (WIPO) | 3+ countries simultaneously | 12-18 months | $3,000-$8,000 for 3 countries |
| Country-by-country | 1-2 specific markets | 6-18 months varies | $1,000-$3,000 per country |
| China direct (CNIPA) | Manufacturing country | 9-12 months | $300-$800 |
| USPTO (US) | US market | 8-12 months | $750-$1,500 |
| EUIPO (EU) | All EU countries | 4-6 months | $1,000-$2,000 |

### Nice Classification

Most CBEC sellers need these classes:

- **Class 9** -- Electronics, phone accessories, chargers
- **Class 11** -- Home appliances, lighting
- **Class 18** -- Bags, luggage, wallets
- **Class 20** -- Furniture, home goods
- **Class 21** -- Kitchen and household utensils
- **Class 25** -- Clothing, footwear
- **Class 28** -- Toys, sporting goods

File the primary class first. Add adjacent classes when budget allows.

### Timeline Planning

```
Month 0:  Select brand name. Run trademark search (TESS, TMview, CNIPA).
Month 1:  File trademark in primary market + China.
Month 2:  Begin brand content creation (logo, guidelines, A+ drafts).
Month 4:  Apply for brand registry (if using intent-to-use or pending mark
          where accepted).
Month 6:  File in expansion markets if growth on track.
Month 9+: Trademark registration completes. Full brand registry access.
```

> For full IP protection guidance, see `references/ip-protection.md`.

## Brand Store / Storefront Design Principles

### Structure

A brand store should have 3-5 pages:

1. **Home Page** -- Hero banner, value proposition, best sellers, category
   navigation.
2. **Category Pages** -- One per product line. Grid or lifestyle layout.
3. **Best Sellers Page** -- Social proof driven. Show ratings, review counts.
4. **About Us Page** -- Brand story, mission, quality commitment.
5. **New Arrivals Page** (optional) -- Keep the store feeling fresh.

### Design Rules

1. **Consistent visual identity.** Same color palette, typography, and photo
   style across all pages.
2. **Mobile-first.** 70%+ of marketplace traffic is mobile. Test every page
   on mobile before publishing.
3. **Above-the-fold impact.** The hero banner must communicate "who you are"
   and "why buy from you" in under 3 seconds.
4. **Lifestyle over product shots.** Use 60% lifestyle imagery, 40% product
   cutouts.
5. **Clear CTAs.** Every section should have an obvious next action (shop
   now, explore category, see all).
6. **Refresh quarterly.** Update banners for seasonal events and new product
   launches.

### A+ Content Best Practices

- Use comparison charts to upsell within your own brand catalog.
- Lead with the #1 customer benefit, not the feature.
- Include "What's in the box" modules to set expectations and reduce returns.
- Use alt text on all images for accessibility and SEO.
- A/B test A+ content using Amazon's Manage Your Experiments tool.

## Brand Storytelling Framework

Use the **Origin-Mission-Proof** framework:

### 1. Origin

Answer: "Why does this brand exist?"

- What problem did the founder encounter?
- What gap in the market was identified?
- Keep it authentic and specific. Generic origin stories build zero trust.

### 2. Mission

Answer: "What does this brand stand for?"

- One sentence. No jargon.
- Example: "We make outdoor gear that lasts a lifetime so you buy less and
  explore more."
- The mission should imply a quality standard or value system.

### 3. Proof

Answer: "Why should I believe you?"

- Customer review highlights and counts.
- Awards, certifications, or test results.
- Specific numbers: "tested to 10,000 cycles", "rated 4.7 across 12,000
  reviews".
- Media mentions or influencer endorsements (if genuine).

### Applying the Framework

| Surface | Origin | Mission | Proof |
|---------|--------|---------|-------|
| A+ Content | Short paragraph | Tagline banner | Review stats, certifications |
| Brand Store About Page | Full story | Mission statement | Testimonials, media logos |
| Product Insert | QR to story | One-liner | Review invite CTA |
| Social Media Bio | One sentence | Hashtag | Follower/review count |
| Packaging | N/A | Tagline | Certification badges |

## When NOT to Invest in Branding Yet

Hold off on brand investment if any of these are true:

1. **You have fewer than 2 SKUs.** A single-product "brand" is fragile and
   not worth the trademark and content investment yet.
2. **Sales are inconsistent.** If monthly revenue swings more than +/-40%,
   you have a demand problem, not a branding problem.
3. **Gross margin is under 25%.** Branding costs 3-5% of revenue. At thin
   margins, this erodes profitability.
4. **You have not validated product-market fit.** If return rate is above 8%
   or review rating is below 4.0, fix the product first.
5. **You are reselling other brands.** You cannot build brand equity on
   products you do not control. Switch to private label first.
6. **You are in a pure commodity category with no differentiation
   opportunity.** Some categories (basic cables, generic screen protectors)
   have near-zero brand premium potential.

**What to do instead:** Focus on product quality, listing optimization, and
review generation. Revisit branding when you meet the decision tree criteria.

## Red Flags

1. **Paying $5,000+ for a "brand package" from a branding agency before
   having product-market fit.** This is burning cash. Build the brand
   incrementally from proven sales data.

2. **Choosing a brand name that is too generic, too similar to existing
   trademarks, or impossible to trademark.** Run a proper trademark search
   (TESS for US, TMview for EU, CNIPA for China) before committing to a
   name.

3. **Ignoring China trademark filing.** Trademark squatting in China is
   extremely common for CBEC sellers. File in China early even if you do
   not sell there. Cost is low ($300-$800), risk of not filing is high.

4. **Building a brand store but never driving traffic to it.** A brand store
   with zero visits is wasted effort. Pair store creation with Sponsored
   Brand ads and external traffic campaigns.

5. **Treating brand registry as a one-time task.** Brand protection is
   ongoing. Monitor for hijackers weekly. Use automated tools (Transparency
   barcodes, Project Zero) where available. Set a calendar reminder for
   trademark renewal dates.
