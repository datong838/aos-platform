## 4. How to Calculate Each Line Item

### Revenue Lines

**Gross Sales:** Total selling price multiplied by units sold. Pull from platform
order reports; do not use payout amounts (those are net of fees).

**Refunds & Chargebacks:** Total amount refunded to customers plus any chargeback
amounts. Includes full refunds, partial refunds, and A-to-Z / buyer protection
claims. Source: platform returns/refund reports.

**Promotional Discounts:** The discount amount absorbed by the seller (not the
platform). Includes percentage-off promotions, buy-one-get-one, and loyalty
discounts. Source: promotional performance reports.

**Coupon Redemptions:** Face value of coupons redeemed. This is the discount
amount, not the clip fee (which goes under Variable Selling Costs).

### COGS Lines

**Factory/Purchase Price:** The per-unit price paid to the supplier. Use the
actual purchase order price, not a quoted price. If prices vary by order
quantity, use the actual price for the specific order that produced the units
being sold. Convert to selling currency at the exchange rate on the date of
payment to the supplier.

**Packaging & Labeling:** Cost of retail-ready packaging, FNSKU/barcode labels,
poly bags, and any insert cards. Include both materials and labor.

**Quality Inspection:** Third-party inspection cost divided by order quantity.
Typically $200-400 per inspection for 1,000-5,000 unit orders. If inspections
are done per shipment, allocate per unit in that shipment.

### Logistics Lines

**Inbound Freight:** Total shipping cost from factory to destination warehouse
divided by units shipped. Include origin charges, ocean/air freight, destination
charges, and drayage. For sea freight, include container costs, port fees, and
inland transport.

**Customs Duties:** Calculated as: (Declared Value + Freight + Insurance) x Duty
Rate. The duty rate depends on the HS code and destination country. Use actual
duty paid from customs entry documents, not estimated rates.

**Customs Brokerage:** Flat fee per entry (typically $100-250 for US imports)
divided by units in the shipment. Some brokers charge per line item on the
entry; account for this if importing multiple products in one shipment.

### Platform Fee Lines

**Referral Fee:** Percentage of the selling price charged by the platform.
Amazon US: 8-15% depending on category. Shopee: 1-6% depending on market
and seller tier. Always use actual fee charged, not the published rate (some
categories have different rates for portions of the price).

**Fulfillment Fee:** Per-unit fee for pick, pack, and ship. Based on product
dimensions and weight. Verify the tier assignment is correct by checking
product dimensions against the platform's tier thresholds. Incorrect tier
assignment is one of the most common fee overcharges.

**Storage Fee:** Monthly fee based on cubic feet (Amazon US) or volume occupied.
Allocate by unit: Total Storage Fee for the month / Average units in warehouse
during that month. Note: rates increase during Q4 (October-December) on Amazon.

### Advertising Lines

**PPC/Sponsored Spend:** Total ad spend for campaigns attributed to this SKU.
Include Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, and any DSP
campaigns. Source: advertising reports with SKU-level attribution.

### Returns Lines

**Returns Processing Cost:** Includes the return shipping cost (if seller-paid),
restocking labor, and repackaging cost. For FBA: the returns processing fee
plus the value of inventory that cannot be resold.

**Inventory Write-Off:** Units returned in unsalvageable condition. Value at
full COGS plus allocated logistics cost. Source: removal order reports or
disposal reports.

### Tax Lines

**VAT/GST:** Only include here if the seller absorbs the tax (prices are
VAT-inclusive and VAT is not shown as a separate line to the customer). If
VAT is added on top of the selling price and collected from the customer,
it flows through the revenue and tax remittance lines, not the P&L cost.

**Income Tax:** Estimated based on the business's effective tax rate. For
planning purposes, use the anticipated rate for the fiscal year. Adjust
quarterly as actual results become clearer.

## 5. Worked Example: SKU P&L for an Amazon US Product

**Product:** Silicone kitchen utensil set (5 pieces)
**Market:** Amazon US (FBA)
**Period:** January (one month)
**Units sold:** 500
**Selling price:** $29.99

```
REVENUE
  Gross Sales (500 x $29.99) ....................... $14,995.00
  minus Refunds (15 units x $29.99) ................ ($449.85)
  minus Coupons (50 redemptions x $3.00) ........... ($150.00)
  = NET REVENUE .................................... $14,395.15

COST OF GOODS SOLD
  Factory Price (500 x $3.20) ...................... $1,600.00
  Packaging (500 x $0.45) ......................... $225.00
  QC Inspection ($300 / 2,000 unit order x 500) .... $75.00
  = TOTAL COGS ..................................... $1,900.00

LOGISTICS & IMPORT
  Sea Freight ($2,800 / 2,000 units x 500) ......... $700.00
  Freight Insurance ................................ $35.00
  Customs Duty (8.5% x declared value $2,335) ...... $198.48
  Customs Brokerage ($180 / 2,000 x 500) ........... $45.00
  = TOTAL LOGISTICS ................................ $978.48

GROSS PROFIT ....................................... $11,516.67
GROSS MARGIN ....................................... 80.0%

PLATFORM FEES
  Referral Fee (15% x $14,395.15) .................. $2,159.27
  FBA Fulfillment Fee (500 x $5.40) ................ $2,700.00
  Storage Fee (est. 500 units avg x $0.87/cbft) .... $130.50
  Professional Seller Subscription (alloc.) ......... $3.33
  = TOTAL PLATFORM FEES ............................ $4,993.10

VARIABLE SELLING COSTS
  PPC Advertising .................................. $1,850.00
  Coupon Clip Fees (50 x $0.60) .................... $30.00
  Payment Processing (2.9% x $14,395.15) ........... $417.46
  Currency Conversion (1.2% of payout) ............. $101.33
  = TOTAL VARIABLE SELLING ......................... $2,398.79

RETURNS & ADJUSTMENTS
  FBA Returns Processing (15 x $3.20) .............. $48.00
  Unsalvageable Write-Off (5 units x $3.65 COGS) ... $18.25
  = TOTAL RETURNS .................................. $66.25

OPERATING PROFIT ................................... $4,058.53
OPERATING MARGIN ................................... 28.2%

TAX & COMPLIANCE
  Estimated Income Tax (22%) ....................... $892.88
  Product Liability Insurance (allocated) .......... $25.00
  = TOTAL TAX & COMPLIANCE ......................... $917.88

NET PROFIT ......................................... $3,140.65
NET MARGIN ......................................... 21.8%

UNIT ECONOMICS
  Units Sold: 500
  Net Revenue per Unit: $28.79
  Net Profit per Unit: $6.28
  TACOS: 12.9% ($1,850 / $14,395.15)
  ACOS: $3.70/unit ($1,850 / 500)
```

**Verdict:** Healthy SKU. Net margin above 20%, TACOS under 15%. The product
can absorb moderate cost shocks (freight increases, FX movement, ad cost
increases) and remain profitable. Continue optimizing.

- Fixed overhead is only 2.1% of revenue -- highly scalable cost structure.
- FX impact is small this month but should be monitored for trend.

## 7. Break-even Analysis Template

### Per-SKU Break-even Price

```
Variable Cost per Unit:
  COGS .......................... $______
  Freight per unit .............. $______
  Duty per unit ................. $______
  Fulfillment fee ............... $______
  Ad cost per unit (at target TACOS) $______
  Returns reserve per unit ...... $______
  Payment processing per unit ... $______
  FX cost per unit .............. $______
  = TOTAL VARIABLE COST ......... $______

Platform Fee Rate: ______% (referral fee as decimal)

Break-even Price = Total Variable Cost / (1 - Platform Fee Rate)

Example:
  Variable cost = $12.50
  Platform fee rate = 15%
  Break-even price = $12.50 / (1 - 0.15) = $12.50 / 0.85 = $14.71
```

Any selling price above $14.71 generates positive contribution margin. But
this is only the variable break-even -- it does not cover fixed overhead
or provide profit.

### Target-Margin Price

```
Target Price = Total Variable Cost / (1 - Platform Fee Rate - Target Margin%)

Example:
  Variable cost = $12.50
  Platform fee rate = 15%
  Target net margin = 20%
  Target price = $12.50 / (1 - 0.15 - 0.20) = $12.50 / 0.65 = $19.23
```

### Monthly Break-even Units

```
Monthly Fixed Costs:
  Software subscriptions ........ $______
  Staff / VA costs .............. $______
  Accountant / professional ..... $______
  Office / workspace ............ $______
  Other fixed costs ............. $______
  = TOTAL MONTHLY FIXED COSTS ... $______

Contribution Margin per Unit = Selling Price - Variable Cost - Platform Fees

Break-even Units = Monthly Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin per Unit

Example:
  Monthly fixed costs = $1,800
  Contribution margin per unit = $8.50
  Break-even units = 1,800 / 8.50 = 212 units/month
```

### Investment Payback Break-even

For new product launches, calculate how many units must be sold to recover
the upfront investment.

```
Launch Investment:
  Initial inventory order ......... $______
  Product photography ............. $______
  Listing creation / A+ content ... $______
  Trademark registration .......... $______
  Initial sample orders ........... $______
  Product testing / compliance .... $______
  Launch advertising budget ....... $______
  = TOTAL LAUNCH INVESTMENT ....... $______

Payback Units = Launch Investment / Net Profit per Unit

Example:
  Launch investment = $8,500
  Net profit per unit = $6.28
  Payback units = 8,500 / 6.28 = 1,354 units

At 500 units/month, payback = 2.7 months
```

## 9. Profitability Segmentation

Analyze profitability across three dimensions to find where value is created
and destroyed.

### By SKU (Pareto Analysis)

Rank all SKUs by net profit contribution (absolute dollars, not margin
percentage). In most cross-border businesses:
- Top 20% of SKUs generate 70-80% of total profit
- Bottom 20% of SKUs are often breakeven or loss-making
- The middle 60% generate modest but positive returns

**Actions by segment:**

| Segment | Definition | Action |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Stars | Top 20% by profit | Invest: increase inventory, expand to new markets, defend ranking |
| Performers | Middle 60% by profit, margin > 10% | Optimize: reduce costs, improve conversion, maintain |
| Marginal | Margin 5-10% | Fix: 60-day plan to improve margin or discontinue |
| Losers | Margin < 5% or negative | Discontinue: liquidate inventory, redeploy capital to Stars |

### By Market

Compare profitability across selling countries/regions.

```
Market       Revenue    Net Profit    Net Margin    Capital Deployed    ROIC
US           $______    $______       ______%       $______             ______%
UK           $______    $______       ______%       $______             ______%
EU (DE)      $______    $______       ______%       $______             ______%
Japan        $______    $______       ______%       $______             ______%
Australia    $______    $______       ______%       $______             ______%
TOTAL        $______    $______       ______%       $______             ______%
```

ROIC (Return on Invested Capital) = Annual Net Profit / Average Capital Deployed
in that market (inventory at cost + prepaid expenses + receivables). This is the
most important metric for capital allocation decisions between markets.

### By Channel / Platform

If selling on multiple platforms (Amazon, Shopee, own website, Walmart, etc.),
compare profitability by channel.

```
Channel      Revenue    Net Profit    Net Margin    Fee Rate    TACOS
Amazon US    $______    $______       ______%       ______%     ______%
Amazon UK    $______    $______       ______%       ______%     ______%
Shopee SG    $______    $______       ______%       ______%     ______%
Own Website  $______    $______       ______%       ______%     ______%
TOTAL        $______    $______       ______%       ______%     ______%
```

Own-website typically has higher gross margin (no referral fee) but higher
customer acquisition cost and lower conversion rate. The net profitability
comparison determines where to invest growth capital.

**Decision framework:** Invest additional capital in the channel with the
highest ROIC, not the highest revenue. A $100,000 channel with 25% ROIC
deserves more capital than a $500,000 channel with 8% ROIC, until the
smaller channel's ROIC diminishes with scale.
