## 1. US Sales Tax

### Nexus Rules

Sales tax in the US is governed at the state level. A seller has a sales tax
obligation in a state when they have "nexus" (sufficient connection) there.
Two types of nexus apply to cross-border sellers:

**Physical Nexus:**
- Inventory stored in a state (including FBA warehouses) creates physical nexus
- Amazon FBA sellers potentially have nexus in every state where Amazon stores
  their inventory (currently 25-30+ states)
- Using a 3PL warehouse creates nexus in that state
- Having employees, contractors, or an office in a state

**Economic Nexus:**
- Most states have adopted economic nexus thresholds (post South Dakota v.
  Wayfair, 2018)
- Common threshold: $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions in a state per year
- Some states have lower thresholds: $100,000 only (no transaction count)
- Thresholds are typically measured on a trailing 12-month or current/prior
  calendar year basis

### Marketplace Facilitator Laws

As of 2025, nearly all US states with sales tax have marketplace facilitator
laws. Under these laws:

- **The marketplace (Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Etsy, etc.) collects and remits
  sales tax** on behalf of third-party sellers for orders fulfilled through
  the marketplace
- The seller is generally relieved of the collection obligation for marketplace
  sales in these states
- **However:** sellers may still need to register in states where they have
  nexus, even if the marketplace collects the tax
- **Filing obligations may remain** even if the marketplace collects; some
  states require a $0 return to be filed

### Registration Requirements

1. Determine where you have nexus (physical and economic)
2. Register for a sales tax permit in each nexus state before making sales
3. File returns on the required schedule (monthly, quarterly, or annually)
4. Even if the marketplace collects, registration may be required for:
   - Direct-to-consumer sales (own website)
   - Wholesale/B2B sales
   - State compliance verification

### Key States to Watch

| State | Threshold | Filing Frequency | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|-------|
| California | $500,000 in sales | Quarterly | Highest threshold; large market |
| Texas | $500,000 in sales | Monthly/Quarterly | No state income tax; major FBA hub |
| New York | $500,000 + 100 transactions | Quarterly | Complex local tax rates |
| Florida | $100,000 in sales | Monthly/Quarterly | No state income tax |
| Washington | $100,000 in sales | Monthly/Quarterly | High compliance expectations |
| Pennsylvania | $100,000 in sales | Monthly/Quarterly | Many FBA warehouses |

### Practical Steps

1. Use a sales tax automation service (TaxJar, Avalara, Vertex) to track
   nexus, calculate rates, and prepare filings
2. Register in nexus states before or immediately upon triggering nexus
3. Set up monthly reconciliation of marketplace-collected tax vs. your records
4. Budget $100-300/month for sales tax automation software
5. Budget $1,000-3,000/year for sales tax professional review

## 2. EU VAT

### Overview

VAT (Value Added Tax) applies to all goods sold to consumers in the EU.
Cross-border sellers must understand two main scenarios:

**Scenario A: Goods shipped directly from outside the EU to EU consumers**
- Shipments valued under EUR 150: eligible for IOSS (Import One-Stop Shop)
- Shipments valued over EUR 150: standard import procedure; buyer pays import
  VAT and duties at customs

**Scenario B: Goods stored in EU warehouses (e.g., Amazon FBA EU)**
- Seller must register for VAT in every EU country where inventory is stored
- VAT is charged at the rate of the destination country
- OSS (One-Stop Shop) simplifies cross-border B2C sales within the EU

### IOSS (Import One-Stop Shop)

For direct shipments under EUR 150:
- Register for IOSS in one EU member state (the identification member state)
- Charge VAT at the destination country rate at point of sale
- File a single monthly IOSS return covering all EU member states
- Goods clear customs without import VAT being charged to the buyer
- Improves customer experience (no surprise charges at delivery)

**Registration process:**
1. Appoint a fiscal representative in an EU member state (required for
   non-EU businesses)
2. Apply for IOSS registration through the fiscal representative's member state
3. Receive IOSS identification number (format: IMxxxxxxxxxxxx)
4. Provide IOSS number to customs declarants and carriers
5. Begin collecting VAT at destination rates

**Filing:** Monthly return due by the end of the month following the reporting
period. Example: January sales reported by end of February.

### OSS (One-Stop Shop)

For goods already stored in the EU and sold to consumers in other EU countries:
- Register for OSS in the country where inventory is stored
- Report cross-border B2C sales through quarterly OSS returns
- Pay VAT due to other member states through a single payment

**Important:** OSS does not replace the requirement to register for VAT in
countries where you store inventory. It only simplifies the reporting of
cross-border sales from those countries.

### VAT Registration in Storage Countries

If using Amazon FBA in Europe, inventory may be stored in multiple countries
through Amazon's Pan-European or European Fulfillment Network programs.

**Common storage countries for Amazon FBA:**
- Germany (DE) -- central hub, almost always used
- France (FR) -- large market
- Italy (IT) -- growing market
- Spain (ES) -- growing market
- Poland (PL) -- Amazon fulfillment center expansion
- Czech Republic (CZ) -- Amazon fulfillment center expansion
- Netherlands (NL) -- Amazon.nl marketplace

**For each storage country, you must:**
1. Register for a local VAT number
2. File periodic VAT returns (monthly or quarterly depending on country)
3. Maintain local-language invoices (or compliant format)
4. Report intra-community movements of your own goods

### VAT Rates by Country (Standard Rates)

| Country | Standard Rate | Reduced Rate(s) | Filing Frequency |
|---------|:------------:|:----------------:|-----------------|
| Germany | 19% | 7% | Monthly (first 2 years), then monthly or quarterly |
| France | 20% | 5.5%, 10% | Monthly or quarterly |
| Italy | 22% | 4%, 5%, 10% | Monthly or quarterly |
| Spain | 21% | 4%, 10% | Quarterly |
| Netherlands | 21% | 9% | Quarterly |
| Poland | 23% | 5%, 8% | Monthly |
| Czech Republic | 21% | 12% | Monthly or quarterly |
| Sweden | 25% | 6%, 12% | Monthly or quarterly |
| Belgium | 21% | 6%, 12% | Monthly or quarterly |

### Practical Steps

1. Determine your EU selling model (direct ship vs. FBA vs. both)
2. Register for IOSS if doing direct shipments under EUR 150
3. Register for VAT in every country where inventory is stored
4. Register for OSS in one storage country for cross-border B2C sales
5. Use a VAT compliance service (AVASK, Hellotax, Taxually, SimplyVAT)
6. Budget EUR 200-500/month per country for compliance services
7. File returns on time: late filing penalties are automatic in most EU countries

## 3. UK VAT

### Post-Brexit Rules

Since January 1, 2021, the UK operates its own VAT system independent of the EU.

**For goods shipped directly to UK consumers from outside the UK:**
- Consignments valued at or below GBP 135: the overseas seller (or the
  marketplace) must register for UK VAT and charge VAT at point of sale
- Consignments valued above GBP 135: standard import procedure; buyer pays
  import VAT and duties

**For goods stored in the UK (e.g., Amazon FBA UK):**
- VAT registration is required regardless of sales value
- Standard rate: 20%
- Reduced rate: 5% (certain goods like children's car seats, energy-saving
  materials)
- Zero rate: 0% (certain food, children's clothing, books)

### Registration Threshold

- **Domestic sales threshold:** GBP 90,000 (updated from GBP 85,000 in April
  2024) in trailing 12 months
- **Overseas sellers storing goods in the UK:** must register regardless of
  threshold if goods are in the UK at the point of sale
- **Marketplace facilitator rules:** Amazon, eBay, and other marketplaces
  collect and remit VAT on behalf of overseas sellers for goods shipped from
  outside the UK valued at or below GBP 135, and for goods stored in the UK
  by overseas sellers

### Flat Rate Scheme

Small businesses with taxable turnover under GBP 150,000 can apply for the
Flat Rate Scheme:
- Pay a fixed percentage of gross turnover as VAT (rate depends on business
  type; typically 7.5-14.5% for retail categories)
- Cannot reclaim input VAT on most purchases
- Simplifies administration but may result in higher effective VAT cost
- Useful for sellers with low input VAT (e.g., importing from outside the UK
  where COGS has no UK VAT)

### Filing Frequency

- Standard: quarterly VAT returns (four per year)
- Monthly filing available by request (useful if regularly in a repayment
  position due to zero-rated exports)
- Making Tax Digital (MTD): digital record-keeping and filing through
  compatible software is mandatory

### Practical Steps

1. Register for UK VAT if storing goods in the UK or exceeding the threshold
2. Determine if the Flat Rate Scheme is beneficial for your situation
3. Set up MTD-compatible accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent)
4. File quarterly returns by the deadline (one month and seven days after
   the end of the VAT quarter)
5. Budget GBP 100-300/month for UK VAT compliance

## 4. Japan Consumption Tax

### JCT Invoice System

Japan's Consumption Tax (JCT) rate is 10% (8% for certain food and beverage
items). The Qualified Invoice System (effective October 2023) significantly
impacts cross-border sellers.

**Key requirements:**
- Businesses must register as Qualified Invoice Issuers to issue compliant
  invoices
- Buyers can only claim input tax credits on purchases from registered issuers
- Registration number format: T + 13 digits

**For cross-border sellers on Amazon Japan:**
- Amazon Japan is a marketplace facilitator for JCT on goods shipped directly
  from overseas to Japan
- For goods stored in Amazon FBA Japan, the seller must register for JCT if
  taxable sales exceed JPY 10,000,000 in the base period (two fiscal years
  prior)
- New market entrants: exempt for the first two fiscal years unless capital
  exceeds JPY 10,000,000

### Registration Process

1. Appoint a tax representative (tax agent) in Japan
2. Submit registration application to the local tax office
3. Obtain JCT registration number
4. Begin charging JCT and issuing qualified invoices
5. File annual JCT returns (with optional interim monthly/quarterly payments)

### Filing

- Annual return due within two months after the end of the fiscal year
- Interim payments: required if annual JCT liability exceeds JPY 480,000
  (monthly, quarterly, or semi-annual interim options)
- Simplified filing available for businesses with taxable sales under
  JPY 50,000,000

## 5. Australia GST

### Registration Requirements

**Threshold:** AUD 75,000 in annual turnover from sales connected to Australia.

**What creates an obligation:**
- Goods stored in Australia (e.g., Amazon FBA Australia)
- Goods shipped directly to Australian consumers from overseas if the seller's
  turnover exceeds the threshold
- Australia's "low-value goods" rules: overseas sellers must register, collect,
  and remit GST on goods valued at AUD 1,000 or less sold to Australian
  consumers

**Rate:** 10% flat rate on most goods and services. Some items are GST-free
(basic food, certain medical supplies, certain educational courses).

### Marketplace Facilitator Rules

Electronic distribution platforms (including Amazon Australia) are responsible
for collecting and remitting GST on sales of low-value imported goods (AUD
1,000 or less) made through their platform by overseas sellers. The seller
does not need to separately collect GST on these marketplace-facilitated sales.

For goods stored in Australia (FBA), the seller is responsible for GST
registration and compliance.

### Registration Process

1. Apply for an Australian Business Number (ABN) via the Australian Business
   Register
2. Register for GST through the ABN application or separately
3. Non-resident sellers without an ABN can register for simplified GST
   registration (for direct-to-consumer online sales only)

### Filing

- Quarterly BAS (Business Activity Statement) is the standard filing period
- Monthly BAS available if turnover exceeds AUD 20,000,000 or by choice
- Annual option available for businesses with turnover under AUD 10,000,000
- Simplified reporting for non-resident sellers using the simplified
  registration

## 6. Singapore GST

### Registration Requirements

**Current threshold (2024+):** SGD 1,000,000 in global turnover for overseas
vendors making supplies to non-GST-registered customers in Singapore. The
previous SGD 100,000 threshold for local businesses still applies for businesses
with a physical presence.

**Rate:** 9% (increased from 8% in January 2024).

**Overseas Vendor Registration (OVR):**
- Applies to overseas sellers making B2C supplies of low-value goods (value not
  exceeding SGD 400) to customers in Singapore
- Also applies to electronic marketplaces that facilitate such supplies
- Amazon Singapore acts as a marketplace facilitator and collects GST on behalf
  of overseas sellers for qualifying transactions

### Registration Process

1. Apply for GST registration with IRAS (Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore)
2. Non-resident businesses can register under the OVR regime
3. Simplified registration available for overseas vendors (cannot claim input
   tax credits)

### Filing

- Quarterly GST returns (standard)
- Monthly or semi-annual filing available by arrangement
- Returns due one month after the end of the accounting period

## 7. Income Tax Considerations

### Business Structure Impact

The business entity type determines how income from cross-border e-commerce
is taxed.

**Common structures for cross-border sellers:**

| Structure | Tax Treatment | Common For | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|-------|
| Sole Proprietorship | Pass-through to personal tax | Small/starting sellers | Simple but unlimited liability |
| LLC (US) | Pass-through or corporate election | US-based sellers | Flexible; state filing requirements |
| Ltd (UK) | Corporation tax (25%) | UK-based sellers | Lower rate (19%) for small profits up to GBP 50,000 |
| Hong Kong Company | 8.25% on first HKD 2M, 16.5% above | Asia-based sellers | Territorial tax system; only HK-sourced income taxed |
| Singapore PTE Ltd | 17% (with exemptions for startups) | Asia-based sellers | Partial tax exemption for first SGD 200,000 |
| US C-Corp | 21% federal + state | Larger US operations | No pass-through; double taxation on dividends |

### Transfer Pricing Basics

If operating through multiple entities in different countries (for example,
a Hong Kong sourcing company and a US selling entity), transfer pricing rules
apply.

**Key principles:**
- Transactions between related entities must be at arm's length (market price)
- Documentation requirements vary by country but generally increase with size
- Penalties for non-compliance can be severe (20-40% penalty on adjustments
  in many jurisdictions)
- Common intercompany transactions: purchase of goods, management fees,
  IP licensing

**For small sellers:** If all operations are through a single entity, transfer
pricing is not typically a concern. It becomes relevant when you set up a
separate sourcing entity, a holding company, or operate in multiple
jurisdictions through related companies.

### Double Taxation Treaties

Tax treaties between countries can reduce withholding taxes and prevent the
same income from being taxed twice. Key considerations:
- Check if a tax treaty exists between your home country and each selling
  country
- Understand the treaty's provisions for business profits, royalties, and
  service fees
- Some treaties reduce withholding tax rates on cross-border payments
- Treaty benefits usually require proper documentation and claims

## 9. Record-Keeping Requirements

### Universal Requirements (All Jurisdictions)

Maintain the following records for a minimum of 5 years (7 years recommended):

**Sales Records:**
- Complete order-level transaction data (order ID, date, customer location,
  item, quantity, price, tax collected)
- Refund and return records with reasons
- Promotional discount records
- Platform settlement reports

**Purchase Records:**
- Supplier invoices (original language and translated if needed)
- Purchase orders and contracts
- Payment records (bank transfers, payment platform confirmations)
- Quality inspection reports

**Import/Export Records:**
- Customs entry documents (entry number, HS codes, declared values)
- Duty payment receipts
- Commercial invoices used for customs
- Bills of lading / airway bills
- Certificates of origin (if claiming preferential duty rates)

**Financial Records:**
- Bank statements (all business accounts, all currencies)
- Payment provider statements (Payoneer, WorldFirst, etc.)
- Credit card statements (business cards)
- Currency conversion records with rates applied

**Tax Records:**
- VAT/GST registration certificates
- Filed tax returns (copies)
- Tax payment receipts
- Correspondence with tax authorities
- Tax advisor communications and advice received

### Record Format

- Electronic records are acceptable in all major jurisdictions
- Original format preferred (PDF invoices, CSV exports, XML data)
- Organize by tax period and jurisdiction
- Back up regularly (cloud storage with versioning recommended)
- Records must be accessible within a reasonable time if requested by
  authorities (typically 30 days)

### Platform-Specific Record Downloads

Download and archive these reports monthly (platforms may only retain data
for 2 years):

| Platform | Key Reports | Retention |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| Amazon | Settlement, Transaction, Inventory, Returns, Tax Document Library | 2 years online |
| Shopee | Income, Order, Return, Settlement | 1-2 years online |
| Walmart | Payment, Transaction, Returns | 2 years online |
| eBay | Transaction, Payout, Tax Invoice | 2 years online |

## 10. When to Get Professional Tax Advice

### Mandatory Situations (Do Not Self-Navigate)

Get professional tax advice before proceeding in these situations:

1. **First-time VAT/GST registration in any country.** The registration
   process has implications for filing frequency, scheme selection, and
   retroactive liability. A professional ensures correct setup from the start.

2. **Receiving a tax authority notice, inquiry, or audit notification.** Never
   respond to a tax authority without professional guidance. The initial
   response sets the tone and scope of the engagement.

3. **Setting up a multi-entity business structure.** Entity selection,
   jurisdiction choice, and intercompany arrangements have long-term tax
   implications that are expensive to reverse. Get advice before incorporation.

4. **Annual revenue exceeding $250,000 equivalent.** At this scale, the
   complexity of multi-jurisdiction compliance, transfer pricing considerations,
   and available tax optimizations justify professional involvement.

5. **Entering a new market (new country).** Each country has unique registration
   requirements, filing obligations, and compliance nuances. A local advisor
   prevents costly mistakes.

### Recommended (Strongly Advised)

6. **Annual tax planning review.** Even if day-to-day compliance is handled
   internally or by software, an annual review by a cross-border tax specialist
   can identify optimization opportunities and compliance gaps.

7. **Changing business structure.** Converting from sole proprietor to LLC,
   or from LLC to corporation, or adding a foreign entity. Each change has
   tax consequences that vary by jurisdiction.

8. **Significant FX exposure.** If currency movements materially impact your
   tax position (different reporting currencies, gains/losses on conversion),
   a tax advisor can help structure transactions to minimize adverse tax effects.

### What to Look For in a Tax Advisor

- **Specific cross-border e-commerce experience.** Not just "international tax"
  but actual experience with marketplace sellers, FBA structures, and
  multi-jurisdiction compliance.
- **Technology-enabled.** Should use or integrate with automated compliance
  tools, not rely on manual spreadsheets.
- **Transparent pricing.** Fixed-fee or clearly scoped engagements. Avoid
  open-ended hourly arrangements for compliance work.
- **Network.** Able to handle or refer to local advisors in all your selling
  jurisdictions.
- **References.** Ask for client references from other cross-border
  e-commerce sellers of similar size.
