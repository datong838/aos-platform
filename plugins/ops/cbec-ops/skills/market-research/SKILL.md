# Market Research: Evaluate and Rank Target Markets

Help sellers decide **where** to sell before they decide **what** to sell.
Market research is the first gate in the cross-border e-commerce lifecycle.
A wrong market choice burns capital on compliance, inventory positioning, and
advertising that cannot be recovered. A right market choice compounds every
downstream decision.

## Core Principle (Iron Law)

> **Never enter a market because it is big. Enter because you have a measurable
> advantage: lower landed cost, higher willingness-to-pay, weaker local
> competition, or existing logistics infrastructure.**

Size alone is a trap. The US Amazon marketplace is the largest in GMV, but it
also has the highest advertising costs, the most entrenched competitors, and the
most complex tax obligations. A smaller market where you hold a structural
advantage will almost always outperform a larger market where you are average.

Every market evaluation must answer one question: **"What is my specific,
defensible edge in THIS market?"** If the answer is "none yet", the market is
not ready for entry.

## 5-Factor Market Scoring Framework

Score each factor 1-5. Multiply by weight. Sum for a composite score (max 25).

| # | Factor               | Weight | What It Measures                                                  |
|---|----------------------|--------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Demand Signal        | 1.0    | Proven buyer intent for your category on target platforms          |
| 2 | Competition Density  | 1.0    | Number and strength of existing sellers in your niche              |
| 3 | Logistics Ease       | 1.0    | Fulfillment infrastructure, shipping cost, delivery time           |
| 4 | Regulatory Friction  | 1.0    | Import duties, certifications, restricted categories, tax burden   |
| 5 | Platform Maturity    | 1.0    | Marketplace ecosystem: traffic, ads tools, seller support, FBA/3PL |

### Scoring Rubric

#### Factor 1: Demand Signal (1-5)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Platform search volume high, category BSR indicates fast turns, Google Trends rising, social proof abundant |
| 4 | Solid platform search volume, category growing, moderate social proof |
| 3 | Demand present but flat or seasonal, limited social proof |
| 2 | Low platform search volume, niche or emerging category |
| 1 | No meaningful platform demand data, speculative market |

**Data sources:** Amazon Brand Analytics / Search Query Performance, Shopee
keyword tools, Google Trends (supplement only -- never sole source), TikTok
Creative Center, Jungle Scout / Helium 10 / Seller Sprite, platform category
BSR rankings.

#### Factor 2: Competition Density (1-5, inverted -- higher is better)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Few established sellers, low review counts (<100 avg), no dominant brand |
| 4 | Moderate sellers, beatable review counts, 1-2 soft brands |
| 3 | Competitive but fragmented, no single seller >15% share |
| 2 | Crowded, multiple sellers with 500+ reviews, 2-3 strong brands |
| 1 | Dominated by 1-2 mega-sellers or brand-gated category |

**Data sources:** Platform search results page 1 analysis, review count
distribution, brand registry presence, sponsored ad density (high ad density =
high competition cost).

#### Factor 3: Logistics Ease (1-5)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | FBA/platform fulfillment available, <5 day delivery, well-developed 3PL network |
| 4 | FBA available or reliable 3PL options, 5-7 day delivery |
| 3 | 3PL available but limited, 7-14 day delivery, moderate shipping cost |
| 2 | Few fulfillment options, 14-21 day delivery, high shipping cost |
| 1 | No reliable fulfillment, >21 day delivery, customs bottlenecks common |

**Key variables:** Warehouse availability near target market, shipping cost per
kg from origin (typically China), customs clearance reliability, last-mile
delivery infrastructure, return logistics cost.

#### Factor 4: Regulatory Friction (1-5, inverted -- higher is better)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Minimal certifications, low import duty, simple tax registration |
| 4 | Standard certifications available, moderate duty, manageable compliance |
| 3 | Specific certifications required (CE, FCC), moderate duty + VAT/GST |
| 2 | Complex certifications, high duty, mandatory local entity or agent |
| 1 | Restricted categories, punitive tariffs, local-entity requirement, uncertain enforcement |

**Common barriers by region:**
- **EU:** CE marking, WEEE, EPR, REACH, VAT OSS registration
- **US:** FCC (electronics), FDA (food/cosmetics), CPSIA (children's), state nexus tax
- **MENA:** Halal certification, ESMA/SASO standards
- **SEA:** Varies widely; Indonesia has SNI, Thailand has TISI
- **Brazil:** INMETRO, Anvisa, punitive import taxes

#### Factor 5: Platform Maturity (1-5)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Mature marketplace with full ad stack, FBA, brand tools, seller support |
| 4 | Growing marketplace with solid ad tools and fulfillment options |
| 3 | Established marketplace but limited ad/fulfillment features |
| 2 | Early-stage marketplace, basic tools only |
| 1 | No established marketplace; must build own storefront/traffic |

### Applying the Framework

**Step 1: Shortlist 3-5 candidate markets** based on the seller's category,
origin country, and existing capabilities.

**Step 2: Score each market** across all 5 factors using real data (not
intuition).

**Step 3: Calculate composite score:**

```
Composite = (Demand x 1.0) + (Competition x 1.0) + (Logistics x 1.0)
          + (Regulatory x 1.0) + (Platform x 1.0)
```

**Step 4: Apply deal-breaker filter.** Any factor scoring 1 is a potential
deal-breaker. If Regulatory Friction = 1, the market requires deep specialist
review before proceeding regardless of composite score.

**Step 5: Rank and recommend.** Present the top 1-2 markets with a clear
rationale tied to the seller's specific advantage.

### Example Scoring

| Factor              | US  | DE  | JP  | SG  |
|---------------------|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| Demand Signal       | 5   | 4   | 4   | 3   |
| Competition Density | 2   | 3   | 3   | 4   |
| Logistics Ease      | 5   | 4   | 4   | 4   |
| Regulatory Friction | 3   | 2   | 3   | 4   |
| Platform Maturity   | 5   | 4   | 4   | 3   |
| **Composite**       | **20** | **17** | **18** | **18** |

In this example, US scores highest on raw demand but lowest on competition. A
seller with differentiated product and ad budget may choose US. A seller with
limited budget and a product requiring no EU certifications may prefer JP or SG
for better competition scores despite lower demand.

## Decision Tree

Use this tree to guide sellers through market evaluation systematically.

```
START: Seller wants to enter a new market
  |
  +--> Q1: Do you have an existing product, or are you choosing product + market together?
  |     |
  |     +--> Existing product --> Go to Q2
  |     +--> No product yet   --> START with cbec-ops:product-selection first,
  |                               then return here with product category defined
  |
  +--> Q2: What is your origin country / manufacturing base?
  |     |
  |     +--> This determines shipping lanes, duty rates, and FTA advantages.
  |          Record: origin country, product HS code, product weight/dimensions.
  |
  +--> Q3: What is your monthly budget for market entry (inventory + ads + compliance)?
  |     |
  |     +--> < $5,000   --> Limit to 1 market, prefer low-barrier (SEA, MX)
  |     +--> $5K-$20K   --> 1-2 markets, consider US/EU if compliance met
  |     +--> > $20K     --> 2-3 markets feasible, full scoring framework
  |
  +--> Q4: Do you have existing logistics infrastructure in any region?
  |     |
  |     +--> Yes --> Score those markets first (Logistics Ease likely 4-5)
  |     +--> No  --> Factor in 3PL setup cost and time
  |
  +--> Q5: Run 5-Factor Scoring on shortlisted markets
  |     |
  |     +--> Score >= 20  --> Strong candidate, proceed to validation
  |     +--> Score 15-19  --> Viable with specific advantages, review weak factors
  |     +--> Score < 15   --> Deprioritize unless strategic long-term play
  |
  +--> Q6: Validate top candidate(s)
  |     |
  |     +--> Check regulatory barriers --> cbec-ops:platform-compliance
  |     +--> Calculate landed cost     --> cbec-ops:pricing-strategy
  |     +--> Verify product-market fit --> cbec-ops:product-selection
  |
  +--> DECISION: Enter market / Defer / Reject
        |
        +--> Enter  --> Proceed to product selection + listing + launch
        +--> Defer  --> Set trigger conditions (e.g., "enter when tariff drops")
        +--> Reject --> Document reasons, revisit in 6 months
```

## Quick Reference: Market Entry Checklist

Use this checklist before committing budget to a new market:

- [ ] Product HS code identified and duty rate calculated for target market
- [ ] Platform-specific demand data collected (not just Google Trends)
- [ ] Competition analysis: page 1 review counts, ad density, brand presence
- [ ] Landed cost calculated including shipping, duties, VAT/GST, platform fees
- [ ] Required certifications identified (CE, FCC, halal, etc.)
- [ ] Fulfillment strategy chosen (FBA, platform fulfillment, 3PL, direct ship)
- [ ] Tax registration requirements understood (VAT OSS, state nexus, GST)
- [ ] Return logistics cost estimated
- [ ] Advertising budget estimated based on platform CPC benchmarks
- [ ] Break-even timeline projected (typically 3-6 months for new market)

## Red Flags and Common Mistakes

### 1. Chasing the US Market by Default

**The mistake:** "Amazon US is the biggest, so we should start there."

**The reality:** US Amazon has the highest CPC ($0.80-$3.00+ depending on
category), the most reviews to overcome, and complex multi-state tax
obligations. Many Chinese sellers lose money in their first year on US Amazon
because they underestimate true FBA + advertising costs.

**The fix:** Calculate full landed cost including FBA fees, advertising at
realistic ACoS (25-40% for launch), returns (5-15%), and compare margin to a
less competitive market.

### 2. Ignoring Import Restrictions and Certifications

**The mistake:** Shipping electronics to the EU without CE marking, or food
products to MENA without halal certification.

**The reality:** Products get seized at customs, listings get suspended, and the
seller loses inventory cost + shipping cost with no recovery path.

**The fix:** Before committing inventory, run the regulatory check via
`cbec-ops:platform-compliance`. Map every certification requirement for your
product category in the target market. Budget $2,000-$10,000+ and 4-12 weeks
for certification if needed.

### 3. Using Google Trends Alone for Demand Validation

**The mistake:** "Google Trends shows rising interest in [category] in
[country], so there must be demand."

**The reality:** Google Trends measures search interest, not purchase intent.
A trending topic does not mean people are buying on e-commerce platforms. The
search volume may be informational, not transactional.

**The fix:** Always validate with platform-specific data: Amazon search volume
(Brand Analytics), Shopee keyword search volume, TikTok Shop trending products.
Platform data reflects actual buyer behavior.

### 4. Confusing GMV with Addressable Market

**The mistake:** "Southeast Asia e-commerce GMV is $100B, so there is huge
opportunity."

**The reality:** GMV includes all categories, all sellers, all platforms.
Your addressable market is the intersection of your specific category, your
target platform, and the price range buyers accept. This is typically 0.01-0.1%
of headline GMV.

**The fix:** Calculate addressable market as: (number of relevant keywords) x
(monthly search volume per keyword) x (conversion rate) x (average selling
price). This gives a realistic revenue ceiling.

### 5. Ignoring Logistics Infrastructure

**The mistake:** Entering a market without understanding last-mile delivery
reliability, return logistics cost, or warehouse availability.

**The reality:** In some Southeast Asian markets, cash-on-delivery (COD) is
dominant (70%+ in Indonesia, Philippines), leading to high rejection rates
(15-30%). In Brazil, delivery to remote areas can take 30+ days. Returns from
MENA markets back to China are often economically unviable.

**The fix:** Score logistics separately. Understand the dominant payment method
(COD vs. prepaid), average delivery time, return rate benchmarks, and cost of
reverse logistics before committing inventory.
