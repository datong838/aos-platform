# Platform Compliance

Protect seller accounts by ensuring every action, listing, and communication complies
with marketplace rules. This skill covers proactive violation prevention, account health
monitoring, appeal preparation, and platform-specific policy navigation.

Compliance is not a one-time checkbox. Platforms update policies continuously, enforcement
algorithms evolve, and the cost of a single suspension can wipe out months of profit.
Treat compliance as a daily operational discipline, not an afterthought.

## The Compliance Iron Law

**No shortcut is worth an account suspension. An account is worth more than any single
product's lifetime profit. When in doubt, do NOT do it.**

This is not a guideline. It is the governing principle behind every decision in this skill.
Every technique, tactic, and optimization suggested elsewhere in the skills suite must pass
through this filter first. If a practice creates meaningful suspension risk, it is off the
table regardless of its potential upside.

## Rationalization Table

Sellers talk themselves into violations through predictable rationalizations. Recognize
these patterns and shut them down:

| Rationalization | Why It Is Wrong |
|----------------|----------------|
| "Everyone does it" | Survivorship bias. You see the survivors, not the thousands of suspended accounts. Platforms enforce in waves; your turn will come. |
| "I'll stop once I'm established" | Your account history is permanent. Violations from day one follow you forever. Platforms can retroactively enforce against historical violations. |
| "It's a gray area" | Gray areas become enforcement targets overnight. Platforms regularly clarify ambiguity by cracking down hard on the exact practice you assumed was safe. |
| "I'll just use a new account" | Multi-account detection is sophisticated and improving constantly. Linked accounts get banned together. You lose everything, not just the violating account. |
| "The policy doesn't explicitly forbid it" | Platforms enforce the spirit of policies, not just the letter. Terms of service grant platforms broad discretion. "Not explicitly forbidden" is not "allowed." |
| "It's a small violation" | Violations compound. A small listing issue plus a minor review issue plus a shipping delay creates a pattern. Algorithms detect patterns, not isolated incidents. |
| "My competitor does it" | Report them. Do not join them. Two wrongs do not make a right, and the platform will eventually catch both of you. Being second to get caught is not a defense. |
| "I need sales now" | Desperation leads to permanent damage. A suspended account generates zero sales forever. Short-term pressure is never a valid reason to risk long-term viability. |

## Zero-Tolerance Violations

These violations are universally prohibited across all major platforms. Committing any of
these typically results in immediate and permanent account suspension with no viable appeal
path.

### Counterfeit Products
- Selling products that infringe trademarks or are marketed as branded goods without
  authorization.
- Using brand names, logos, or imagery without explicit rights.
- "Inspired by" or "compatible with" language does not protect against IP claims if the
  product itself copies protected designs.

### Review Manipulation
- Purchasing, incentivizing, or exchanging reviews in any form.
- Using review services, review groups, or social media exchanges.
- Inserting review request cards that offer compensation (discounts, gifts, refunds).
- Selectively requesting reviews only from satisfied customers.
- Reviewing your own products or having friends/family do so.

### Fake Orders and Inflated Metrics
- Placing orders on your own listings to boost sales rank.
- Using services that generate artificial orders, clicks, or traffic.
- Manipulating any platform metric through non-genuine transactions.

### Multiple Account Abuse
- Operating multiple seller accounts on the same platform without explicit platform
  approval.
- Creating new accounts to circumvent suspensions or restrictions.
- Using different identities, addresses, or payment methods to disguise linked accounts.

### Buyer Information Misuse
- Using buyer contact information for purposes outside the specific transaction.
- Adding buyers to marketing lists without explicit consent.
- Sharing buyer data with third parties.
- Contacting buyers through channels outside the platform's messaging system.

### Safety and Regulatory Fraud
- Selling products that fail to meet mandatory safety standards.
- Falsifying compliance certificates, test reports, or regulatory documents.
- Ignoring product recall notices or safety alerts.

## Account Health Monitoring Workflow

### Daily Checks (5 minutes)

1. **Review account health dashboard** -- Check for new policy violations, warnings,
   or performance alerts.
2. **Scan messages** -- Respond to any buyer messages or platform notifications within
   the required timeframe (usually 24 hours).
3. **Check order defects** -- Look for new A-to-Z claims, chargebacks, negative feedback,
   or return requests that need immediate attention.
4. **Verify active listings** -- Confirm no listings have been suppressed, deactivated,
   or flagged overnight.

### Weekly Checks (30 minutes)

1. **Performance metrics review** -- Compare current metrics against platform thresholds:
   - Order Defect Rate (target: well below platform maximum)
   - Late Shipment Rate (target: under 4%, ideally under 2%)
   - Valid Tracking Rate (target: above 95%)
   - Customer Response Time (target: within platform requirements)
   - Cancellation Rate (target: under 2.5%)
2. **Policy update scan** -- Check platform seller forums, announcement pages, and
   official communication channels for policy changes.
3. **Listing compliance spot-check** -- Randomly audit 5-10 listings for:
   - Accurate product descriptions matching actual product.
   - Images that comply with current guidelines.
   - No prohibited claims (medical, safety, performance without substantiation).
   - Correct category placement.
4. **Intellectual property review** -- Check for new IP complaints, rights owner claims,
   or takedown notices.
5. **Review and feedback audit** -- Monitor for suspicious review activity (both positive
   and negative) and report any review manipulation targeting your listings.

### Monthly Checks (1-2 hours)

1. **Full catalog compliance audit** -- Review all active listings against current platform
   policies. Flag anything that was compliant last month but may not be under updated rules.
2. **Competitor monitoring** -- Check if competitors are engaging in practices that could
   trigger industry-wide enforcement actions.
3. **Regulatory update review** -- Check for changes in product safety regulations, import
   requirements, or labeling laws in your target markets.
4. **Account documentation audit** -- Ensure all required business documents, tax records,
   and identity verifications are current and not approaching expiration.

## Appeal Letter Framework

When a violation occurs, a well-structured appeal is your best (and sometimes only) chance
at reinstatement. Follow this four-step framework.

### Step 1: Acknowledge

State clearly what happened without deflecting, minimizing, or blaming the platform. Show
that you understand exactly which policy was violated and why the platform took action.

**Do**:
- Reference the specific policy by name or section.
- Accept responsibility directly.
- Use factual, professional language.

**Do not**:
- Blame the platform, buyers, or competitors.
- Claim the violation was unfair or a mistake (unless you have concrete evidence).
- Use emotional language or threats.

### Step 2: Explain Root Cause

Identify the specific operational failure that led to the violation. The platform wants to
see that you understand not just what happened, but why it happened at the process level.

**Good root causes**:
- "Our quality control process did not include [specific check], which allowed [specific
  issue] to reach customers."
- "We lacked a systematic review of [specific area], leading to [specific gap]."
- "Our team member responsible for [task] was not trained on [specific policy requirement]."

**Bad root causes**:
- "It was a one-time mistake." (Not a root cause.)
- "Our supplier sent the wrong product." (Deflects responsibility.)
- "We didn't know about the policy." (Ignorance is not a defense.)

### Step 3: Corrective Action (Already Taken)

Describe the specific, concrete actions you have already completed to fix the immediate
problem. Use past tense to demonstrate these are done, not planned.

- "We have removed all affected listings as of [date]."
- "We have issued full refunds to all affected customers."
- "We have disposed of all non-compliant inventory."
- "We have retrained our team on [specific policy] on [date]."

### Step 4: Preventive Measures (Systemic Changes)

Describe the systemic changes you are implementing to ensure this type of violation never
recurs. This is the most important section because it demonstrates long-term reliability.

- New quality control checkpoints (describe each checkpoint specifically).
- Updated standard operating procedures (reference the specific SOP).
- Regular compliance audits (specify frequency and scope).
- Staff training programs (specify content, frequency, and accountability).
- Technology or tooling changes (specify what tools and what they monitor).

### Appeal Formatting Rules

- Keep the appeal under 1000 words. Reviewers process hundreds per day.
- Use bullet points and clear section headers.
- Include specific dates, quantities, and reference numbers.
- Attach supporting documentation (invoices, certificates, screenshots) where relevant.
- Do not submit multiple appeals simultaneously. Wait for a response before escalating.
- If the first appeal is denied, revise with additional specificity. Do not resubmit the
  same letter.

## Pre-Launch Compliance Checklist

Run this checklist before launching any new product or entering any new marketplace. Every
item must pass before proceeding.

### Product Level

- [ ] **Product is not restricted or prohibited** -- Verified against the platform's
  restricted products list and applicable laws in the target market.
- [ ] **Intellectual property is clear** -- No trademark conflicts, no unauthorized brand
  usage, no design patent infringement. If selling branded goods, authorization
  documentation is on file.
- [ ] **Safety and regulatory compliance** -- All required certifications, test reports,
  and compliance marks are obtained and valid (e.g., CE, FCC, CPSC, FDA as applicable).
- [ ] **Labeling requirements met** -- Product labels include all legally required
  information for the target market (origin, materials, warnings, barcodes).
- [ ] **Product claims are substantiated** -- Every claim in the listing (performance,
  safety, health, environmental) is backed by documentation.

### Listing Level

- [ ] **Title follows platform guidelines** -- Character limits, formatting rules, and
  prohibited terms (e.g., "best," "guaranteed," subjective superlatives) are respected.
- [ ] **Images comply with platform rules** -- Main image meets background requirements;
  no prohibited overlays, watermarks, or lifestyle elements where not allowed.
- [ ] **Description is accurate** -- Product description matches the actual product. No
  exaggerations, misleading dimensions, or inaccurate feature claims.
- [ ] **Category and attributes are correct** -- Product is in the correct category with
  accurate attribute values.
- [ ] **No prohibited keywords** -- Backend search terms do not contain competitor brand
  names, ASINs, or misleading terms.

### Account Level

- [ ] **Business documentation is current** -- Business license, tax registration, bank
  account verification, and identity documents are up to date.
- [ ] **Account health metrics are healthy** -- All performance metrics are within
  acceptable ranges before adding new products that could introduce risk.
- [ ] **Platform-specific requirements met** -- Any category-specific approvals (ungating),
  brand registry enrollment, or seller tier requirements are satisfied.

## Red Flags

Watch for these warning signs that compliance risk is building:

**Operational Red Flags**:
- Account health score declining for two or more consecutive weeks.
- Increasing rate of buyer complaints or returns with the same root cause.
- Supplier changing product specifications without notification.
- Team members taking shortcuts on quality checks due to volume pressure.
- Receiving policy warnings on the same issue more than once.

**Market Red Flags**:
- Platform announces a policy crackdown in your category.
- Competitors in your niche are being suspended (enforcement wave incoming).
- New regulations take effect in your target market.
- Media coverage of safety issues with products similar to yours.
- Platform introduces new compliance requirements with a short transition window.

**Listing Red Flags**:
- Unusually high return rate on a specific product (may indicate listing inaccuracy).
- Sudden spike in negative reviews mentioning the same issue.
- Receiving IP complaints from a brand you did not think was related.
- Customer messages asking "is this authentic?" (signals trust issues with your listing).
- Listing suppressed or flagged without a clear notification (check account health
  dashboard proactively).

**Review Red Flags**:
- Sudden influx of positive reviews with similar language patterns (may indicate
  manipulation by a third party attempting to bait your account).
- Competitor leaving fake negative reviews (document and report immediately).
- Review-related warnings from the platform, even if you believe you are innocent.
