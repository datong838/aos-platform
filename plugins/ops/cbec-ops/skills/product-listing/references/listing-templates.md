## Title Templates by Product Type

Each template follows the pattern of front-loading the primary keyword while including
essential product attributes. Adapt the element order and separator style to match
each platform's norms.

### Electronics

**Template**:
```
[Product Type] [Key Tech Feature] [Connectivity/Compatibility] - [Power/Spec], [Form Factor], [Key Benefit]
```

**Examples**:
```
Wireless Charging Pad Qi-Certified Fast Charge - 15W, Ultra Slim, Compatible with iPhone and Samsung Galaxy
Portable Bluetooth Speaker Waterproof IPX7 - 24-Hour Battery, 360 Surround Sound, Built-in Microphone
USB-C Hub Adapter 7-in-1 for MacBook Pro - 4K HDMI, 100W PD Charging, SD Card Reader, USB 3.0 Ports
Noise Cancelling Headphones Over-Ear Wireless - 40-Hour Battery, Hi-Res Audio, Foldable Design for Travel
```

### Apparel

**Template**:
```
[Gender/Audience] [Product Type] [Style/Cut] [Material] - [Key Feature], [Occasion/Use], [Size Note]
```

## Bullet Point Formula

### Core Formula: Feature + Benefit + Proof

Every bullet point should answer three questions:
1. **What is it?** (Feature)
2. **Why should I care?** (Benefit)
3. **Why should I believe you?** (Proof/Detail)

### Template

```
[BENEFIT-DRIVEN HEADER IN CAPS] - [Feature description] that [benefit to the buyer].
[Specific proof point: measurement, material, certification, or comparison].
```

### Framework by Bullet Position

| Position | Focus Area | Template |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| Bullet 1 | Primary USP | [TOP BENEFIT] - The standout reason to choose this product over alternatives. [Proof]. |
| Bullet 2 | Key specification | [TECHNICAL ADVANTAGE] - Key spec that matters to informed buyers. [Specific number or comparison]. |
| Bullet 3 | Quality/durability | [BUILT TO LAST] - Material or construction detail that justifies the price. [Certification or test result]. |
| Bullet 4 | Convenience/ease | [EASY TO USE] - How the product simplifies the buyer's life. [Specific scenario or use case]. |
| Bullet 5 | Value/included items | [COMPLETE PACKAGE] - What the buyer receives and any guarantee. [List contents and warranty]. |

## Description Frameworks

### Framework 1: Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)

Best for products that solve a clear pain point.

**Structure**:
```
[PROBLEM] - State the frustration or pain point the buyer experiences.

[AGITATE] - Amplify the problem. Describe the consequences of not solving it.

[SOLVE] - Introduce the product as the solution. Explain how it eliminates the problem.

[PROOF] - Back up the claim with specifics: materials, specs, testimonials, or guarantees.

[CTA] - Call to action.
```

**Example (Ergonomic Mouse)**:
```
Wrist pain from your mouse should not be the cost of getting work done.

Hours of daily clicking and scrolling strain your wrist at an unnatural angle. Over
time this leads to discomfort, reduced productivity, and potentially chronic issues
that affect your work and personal life.

The ErgoGlide Vertical Mouse positions your hand in a natural handshake grip that
eliminates wrist torsion entirely. Your forearm stays neutral while the sculpted
thumb rest and contoured buttons let you click and scroll with zero strain.

Designed with a 57-degree ergonomic angle validated by physiotherapy research.
Features adjustable DPI (800-2400), silent click switches rated for 10 million
clicks, and USB-C rechargeable battery lasting 90 days per charge.

Choose the ErgoGlide today and feel the difference in your first hour of use.
```

### Framework 2: Feature-Advantage-Benefit (FAB)

Best for products with multiple strong features that need clear benefit connections.

**Structure**:
```
[HEADLINE] - One-sentence product positioning statement.

[FAB Block 1]
Feature: [What it has]
Advantage: [Why that feature matters technically]
Benefit: [How the buyer's life improves]

[FAB Block 2]
(repeat)

[FAB Block 3]
(repeat)

[SUMMARY + CTA]
```

**Example (Air Purifier)**:
```
Clean air in every room, engineered for homes with pets, allergies, and urban pollution.

3-Stage HEPA Filtration
Our H13 True HEPA filter captures 99.97% of particles down to 0.3 microns. This
means pollen, dust mites, pet dander, and smoke particles are removed from your air
in a single pass. Breathe easier and sleep better starting the first night.

Ultra-Quiet Night Mode
At the lowest setting, the motor runs at just 24 dB, quieter than a whisper. Unlike
competitors that hum and rattle, this purifier disappears into the background so you
can sleep without white noise machines.

Smart Air Quality Sensor
The built-in PM2.5 sensor continuously monitors your air and automatically adjusts
fan speed. The LED ring changes color (blue, green, yellow, red) so you can see your
air quality at a glance without checking an app.

Covers rooms up to 450 square feet. Replace the filter every 6-8 months (replacement
filters sold separately). Add one to your cart and breathe the difference.
```

### Framework 3: Story-Based (For Brand-Driven Products)

Best for products where brand origin, craftsmanship, or mission is a key differentiator.

**Structure**:
```
[ORIGIN] - Brief brand or product origin story (2-3 sentences).

[PHILOSOPHY] - What principle guides the product design.

[PRODUCT DETAIL] - Specific features and materials.

[SOCIAL PROOF] - Mention of community, reviews, or recognition.

[CTA]
```

### Framework 4: Use-Case Segmented

Best for products with multiple distinct use cases or audiences.

**Structure**:
```
[OVERVIEW] - One-sentence product description.

For [Audience/Use Case 1]:
[How the product serves this specific need]

For [Audience/Use Case 2]:
[How the product serves this specific need]

For [Audience/Use Case 3]:
[How the product serves this specific need]

[SPECS SUMMARY]
[CTA]
```

## Keyword Integration Techniques

### Technique 1: Natural Sentence Integration

Embed keywords into readable sentences rather than listing them.

**Bad**: "Wireless earbuds bluetooth earbuds TWS earbuds sport earbuds running earbuds"

**Good**: "These wireless earbuds use Bluetooth 5.3 for a stable connection during
running, gym workouts, and everyday commutes."

### Technique 2: Header Keyword Loading

Place keywords in bullet headers and section headings where they carry more weight.

```
WATERPROOF BLUETOOTH SPEAKER FOR OUTDOOR USE - Built with IPX7 waterproof rating...
```

The header contains three valuable keyword phrases: "waterproof bluetooth speaker",
"bluetooth speaker for outdoor", and "outdoor use".

### Technique 3: Specification Keyword Embedding

Technical specifications naturally contain keywords that buyers search for.

```
Capacity: 20000mAh power bank
Output: USB-C PD 30W fast charging
Compatibility: iPhone 15, Samsung Galaxy S24, iPad, MacBook Air
```

Each spec line contains searchable product terms.

### Technique 4: Use-Case Keyword Expansion

Frame features in terms of how buyers search for solutions.

**Feature**: "Foldable design"

**Expanded with use-case keywords**: "Foldable design makes it the ideal travel
charger for business trips and vacations. Fits in laptop bags, carry-on luggage,
and purses."

This captures: "travel charger", "charger for business trips", "charger for laptop bag".

### Technique 5: Synonym Distribution

Distribute synonyms across different listing sections to capture variant searches.

| Section | Term Used |
|---------|-----------|
| Title | Wireless earbuds |
| Bullet 1 | Bluetooth earphones |
| Bullet 3 | TWS headphones |
| Description | In-ear headset |
| Backend | Earpiece, ear pods |

### Keyword Integration Rules

- Each keyword or phrase should appear no more than 2-3 times across the entire listing.
- Never sacrifice readability for keyword density.
- Primary keyword appears in: title (required), first bullet (recommended), description
  (recommended).
- Secondary keywords appear in: title (if space allows), bullets (distribute across all),
  description.
- Tertiary keywords appear in: backend search terms, description.
- Never use competitor brand names as keywords.

## Localization Checklist

When listing the same product across different English-speaking markets, adapt the
copy for regional differences.

### US English vs UK English

| Element | US English | UK English |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Spelling | color, flavor, organize, traveled | colour, flavour, organise, travelled |
| Units | inches, pounds, ounces, Fahrenheit | centimeters, kilograms, grams, Celsius |
| Date format | MM/DD/YYYY | DD/MM/YYYY |
| Clothing sizes | US sizing (S, M, L, 2, 4, 6, 8) | UK sizing (6, 8, 10, 12, 14) |
| Voltage | 110V | 220-240V |
| Plug type | Type A/B (two/three prong) | Type G (three rectangular pins) |
| Currency symbol | $ before number ($29.99) | Pound before number (GBP 29.99) |
| Common terms | "apartment", "truck", "sneakers" | "flat", "lorry", "trainers" |
| Shipping language | "shipping", "zip code" | "delivery", "postcode" |

### SEA English (Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines)

| Element | Adaptation |
|---------|-----------|
| Spelling | Mix of US and UK; either accepted, be consistent within a listing |
| Units | Metric (cm, kg, g, Celsius) |
| Sizing | Often include Asian size charts alongside US/UK sizes |
| Currency | Display in local currency (SGD, MYR, PHP) |
| Language mixing | Common to mix English with local terms; acceptable on Shopee |
| Tone | Slightly more casual and friendly than US/UK; emoji usage accepted |
| Shipping terms | "delivery", "dispatch", "estimated delivery date" common |
| Price anchoring | Emphasize value; "worth [X], now only [Y]" resonates |

### Localization Process

1. **Translate product title**: Do not just translate literally; research which keywords
   local buyers actually search for. A direct translation often misses the
   highest-volume local search term.
2. **Adapt bullet points**: Convert units, adjust sizing references, and modify use-case
   examples to match local context.
3. **Update images**: If infographics contain text, create localized versions. If
   lifestyle images show a clearly non-local setting, consider replacing.
4. **Check compliance**: Labeling, safety certification, and claims requirements differ
   by country. Adjust description claims accordingly.
5. **Review pricing context**: A price that seems reasonable in the US may be premium
   in SEA. Adjust positioning and value messaging.
6. **Test with native speakers**: Even within English markets, phrasing that sounds
   natural in the US can sound awkward in the UK or Singapore.

## A/B Testing Suggestions

Systematic testing improves listing performance over time. Focus on the elements with
the highest impact on CTR and conversion.

### What to Test

| Element | Test Variables | Impact On | Priority |
|---------|---------------|-----------|----------|
| Main image | Background style, angle, props, lifestyle vs product-only | CTR | Highest |
| Title | Keyword order, feature emphasis, structure | CTR + SEO rank | High |
| Price | Price points, psychological pricing ($29.99 vs $30) | Conversion | High |
| Bullet order | Which benefit leads, different benefit emphasis | Conversion | Medium |
| Secondary images | Image sequence, infographic design, number of images | Conversion | Medium |
| Description | PAS vs FAB framework, length, tone | Conversion | Medium |
| Variation naming | Color names (e.g., "Midnight Black" vs "Black") | Conversion | Low |

### A/B Testing Methods by Platform

**Amazon**:
- Use Amazon's Manage Your Experiments (available to Brand Registered sellers).
- Test A+ Content, main image, title, and bullet points.
- Run each test for at least 4 weeks or until statistical significance.
- Test one element at a time to isolate the impact.

**Shopee**:
- No native A/B testing tool. Test sequentially.
- Change one element, run for 1-2 weeks, compare metrics to the previous period.
- Control for external factors (campaigns, seasonality) when analyzing results.
- Use Shopee's analytics dashboard to track click-through and conversion rates.

**TikTok Shop**:
- Test through video content variation (different hooks, formats, creators).
- The product card can be updated; compare performance across time periods.
- Creator A/B testing: send the same product to creators with different styles and
  compare conversion.

### Testing Prioritization Framework

Test in this order for maximum impact:

1. **Main image** -- This is the single element that determines whether a shopper clicks.
   A better main image can lift CTR by 20-50%.
2. **Price and offer** -- Test price points, bundle configurations, and coupon amounts.
3. **Title** -- Test keyword order and feature emphasis. Affects both CTR and search rank.
4. **Bullet points** -- Test different lead benefits and copy angles.
5. **Secondary images** -- Test image sequence and infographic designs.
6. **Description** -- Test frameworks and length (lower priority; fewer shoppers read
   the full description).

### Interpreting Results

| Metric | What It Means | Action |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| CTR increased, conversion unchanged | Title/image improved, but page does not close | Optimize bullets, pricing, or reviews |
| CTR unchanged, conversion increased | Page experience improved | Maintain title/image; continue page optimization |
| CTR increased, conversion decreased | Attracting wrong traffic | Title/image may be misleading; realign with actual product |
| CTR decreased, conversion increased | Filtering to more qualified traffic | Acceptable if total sales increase |
| Both increased | Winning combination | Scale and apply learnings to other listings |
| Both decreased | Revert immediately | Return to previous version |

### Testing Cadence

- Run one test at a time per listing.
- Minimum test duration: 7 days (14 days preferred for statistical reliability).
- Do not test during major promotional events (11.11, Prime Day, etc.) as external
  factors overwhelm test signals.
- Document every test: what changed, when, hypothesis, result, and decision.
- Build a testing log to accumulate learnings across the product catalog.
