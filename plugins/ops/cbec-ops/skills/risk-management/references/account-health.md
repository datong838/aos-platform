## 1. Account Suspension Triggers by Platform

### Amazon

**Immediate Suspension Triggers (zero tolerance):**
- Selling counterfeit products or products reported as counterfeit
- Selling prohibited / restricted items without approval
- Multiple accounts without legitimate business justification
- Manipulating reviews (incentivized reviews, review swaps, purchased reviews)
- Operating from a sanctioned country
- Identity verification failure

**Performance-Based Suspension Triggers:**
- Order Defect Rate (ODR) exceeding 1%
- Late Shipment Rate exceeding 4%
- Pre-Fulfillment Cancellation Rate exceeding 2.5%
- Valid tracking rate below 95%
- Invoice defect rate exceeding thresholds (varies by category)

**Policy Violation Triggers:**
- Intellectual property complaints (patent, trademark, copyright)
- Product authenticity complaints
- Product condition complaints (used sold as new)
- Listing policy violations (prohibited claims, keyword stuffing)
- Restricted product violations (pesticides, supplements without approval)
- Drop shipping policy violations (shipping with third-party branding)

### Shopee

**Immediate Suspension Triggers:**
- Selling counterfeit or prohibited items
- Account linked to previously banned seller
- Fraudulent order activity (fake orders for metrics manipulation)
- Severe chat response violations (abuse, spam)

**Performance-Based Triggers:**
- Shop rating below platform threshold (varies by market, typically < 4.0)
- Late shipment rate above threshold (varies by market, typically > 10%)
- Non-fulfillment rate above threshold (typically > 5%)
- Chat response rate below required minimum (varies by market)
- Return/refund rate above category average by significant margin

**Penalty Point System:**
- Shopee uses a penalty point system that varies by market
- Points accumulate for violations and reset periodically
- Reaching the maximum points threshold triggers suspension
- Severity varies: 1-3 points for minor violations, 6+ for serious violations
- Track points in Seller Centre under Account Health

### TikTok Shop

**Immediate Suspension Triggers:**
- Selling counterfeit or prohibited products
- Misleading product information or images
- Violating community guidelines (inappropriate content)
- Selling regulated items without proper certification
- Identity verification failure or misrepresentation

**Performance-Based Triggers:**
- Order cancellation rate exceeding threshold (typically > 5%)
- Late dispatch rate exceeding threshold (typically > 4%)
- Product quality return rate above threshold
- Low customer satisfaction score (below 4.0 in some markets)
- Intellectual property violation reports

**Content-Specific Triggers:**
- Live stream policy violations (false claims, prohibited content)
- Affiliate program abuse
- Misleading promotions or pricing
- Using copyrighted music/content without license in product videos

## 2. Early Warning Signs

Monitor these metrics trending toward danger zones. Act when a metric enters
the warning zone, not when it crosses the threshold.

### Amazon Early Warning Indicators

| Metric | Safe | Warning | Danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order Defect Rate | < 0.5% | 0.5 - 0.8% | > 0.8% (threshold: 1%) |
| Late Shipment Rate | < 2% | 2 - 3.5% | > 3.5% (threshold: 4%) |
| Cancellation Rate | < 1% | 1 - 2% | > 2% (threshold: 2.5%) |
| Valid Tracking Rate | > 98% | 95 - 98% | < 95% |
| A-to-Z Claim Rate | < 0.1% | 0.1 - 0.3% | > 0.3% |
| Negative Feedback Rate | < 2% | 2 - 4% | > 4% |
| IP Complaints (30 days) | 0 | 1-2 | 3+ |
| Policy Warnings (90 days) | 0 | 1 | 2+ |

### Shopee Early Warning Indicators

| Metric | Safe | Warning | Danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shop Rating | > 4.5 | 4.0 - 4.5 | < 4.0 |
| Late Shipment Rate | < 5% | 5 - 8% | > 8% |
| Non-Fulfillment Rate | < 2% | 2 - 4% | > 4% |
| Chat Response Rate | > 90% | 80 - 90% | < 80% |
| Penalty Points | 0-3 | 4-6 | 7+ |
| Return Rate | < category avg | At category avg | > category avg |

### TikTok Shop Early Warning Indicators

| Metric | Safe | Warning | Danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cancellation Rate | < 2% | 2 - 4% | > 4% |
| Late Dispatch Rate | < 2% | 2 - 3.5% | > 3.5% |
| Return Rate | < 3% | 3 - 5% | > 5% |
| Customer Satisfaction | > 4.5 | 4.0 - 4.5 | < 4.0 |
| Content Violations (30d) | 0 | 1 | 2+ |

### Behavioral Warning Signs (All Platforms)

These qualitative signals often precede metric-based warnings:

- Sudden spike in negative reviews mentioning the same issue
- Increase in A-to-Z claims or equivalent dispute filings
- Customers reporting items not matching listing descriptions
- Shipping carrier experiencing systemic delays
- Supplier quality declining (more QC failures per batch)
- Seasonal demand surge without staffing or process adjustment
- New competitor filing IP complaints against your listings

## 3. Account Health Dashboard Monitoring Routine

### Daily Check (5 minutes)

- [ ] Open account health dashboard on each active platform
- [ ] Check for new policy violations or warnings (red/yellow alerts)
- [ ] Review any new buyer messages requiring urgent response
- [ ] Verify no listings have been suppressed or deactivated overnight
- [ ] Check pending orders for any approaching late shipment deadlines

### Weekly Check (15 minutes)

- [ ] Review all account health metrics against warning thresholds
- [ ] Check IP complaint notifications and respond to any pending cases
- [ ] Review return/refund requests for patterns
- [ ] Audit recent negative reviews for recurring product issues
- [ ] Verify automated systems (repricing, inventory sync) are functioning
- [ ] Check seller rating trend (7-day moving average)

### Monthly Check (30 minutes)

- [ ] Full account health metrics review with month-over-month comparison
- [ ] Review all open cases and their status
- [ ] Audit listing compliance across top 20 SKUs
- [ ] Check for platform policy updates or changes
- [ ] Review account performance against peer benchmarks
- [ ] Verify all required certifications and documents are current
- [ ] Update risk assessment for account category in risk matrix

### Quarterly Check (60 minutes)

- [ ] Full compliance audit of all active listings
- [ ] Review and update product images, descriptions for policy compliance
- [ ] Verify intellectual property registrations are current
- [ ] Assess account diversification status
- [ ] Test backup account readiness (if applicable)
- [ ] Review suspension response playbook and update contacts

## 4. Preventive Measures Checklist

Implement all 20 measures before they are needed. Check off each item and
assign an owner and review date.

### Product and Listing Compliance

- [ ] 1. All product listings match actual product received by customer
  (images, description, dimensions, materials)
- [ ] 2. No prohibited claims in listings (medical claims, false certifications,
  unverified superlatives)
- [ ] 3. All required category-specific certifications uploaded and current
  (FDA, CE, FCC, CPSC, etc.)
- [ ] 4. Product images are original or properly licensed (no watermarks,
  no stolen images)
- [ ] 5. Keywords are relevant and accurate (no competitor brand names,
  no irrelevant keyword stuffing)

### Intellectual Property Protection

- [ ] 6. Brand registered on all selling platforms (Amazon Brand Registry,
  Shopee Brand Portal, TikTok Shop Brand Hub)
- [ ] 7. Trademarks registered in all selling jurisdictions
- [ ] 8. Invoices and authorization letters on file for all branded products
  (if reselling)
- [ ] 9. Proactive monitoring for counterfeit sellers on your listings
- [ ] 10. DMCA / IP complaint response templates prepared

### Operations and Fulfillment

- [ ] 11. Shipping SLAs set conservatively (promise later, deliver earlier)
- [ ] 12. Valid tracking uploaded within platform-required timeframe for all
  orders
- [ ] 13. Return address and return process clearly documented
- [ ] 14. Customer service response within platform SLA (typically < 24 hours)
- [ ] 15. Inventory accuracy maintained (no overselling leading to cancellations)

### Review and Feedback Management

- [ ] 16. No incentivized review programs (even indirect ones like insert cards
  with conditional offers)
- [ ] 17. Product insert cards comply with platform policy (no review
  solicitation that implies incentive)
- [ ] 18. Negative review monitoring with root cause analysis process
- [ ] 19. Legitimate review request program (Amazon "Request a Review" button
  or platform equivalent)

### Account Security and Documentation

- [ ] 20. Two-factor authentication enabled on all seller accounts, email
  accounts, and payment accounts. Backup codes stored securely offline.

## 5. Account Suspension Response Protocol

### First 24 Hours

**Hour 0-1: Assessment**

1. Read the suspension notification completely. Identify:
   - The specific reason code or policy cited
   - The ASIN(s) or listing(s) involved (if applicable)
   - The type of suspension (listing, account, ASIN)
   - Whether an appeal is available and the deadline
2. Do NOT submit an immediate appeal. Rushed appeals have lower success rates.
3. Screenshot and save all suspension-related communications.

**Hour 1-4: Damage Assessment**

4. Calculate financial exposure:
   - Pending payouts held by the platform
   - FBA/warehouse inventory value at risk
   - Open orders that may be cancelled
   - Advertising spend still running (pause immediately)
5. Identify all affected listings and channels.
6. Notify internal team members with relevant responsibilities.

**Hour 4-12: Evidence Gathering**

7. Collect all evidence related to the suspension reason:
   - Supplier invoices showing product authenticity
   - Quality inspection reports
   - Product test reports and certifications
   - Communication records with customers
   - Shipping documentation and tracking data
8. If IP complaint: identify the complainant, review their claim, gather
   proof of authorization or non-infringement.

**Hour 12-24: Plan of Action Drafting**

9. Draft Plan of Action following the structure in Section 6.
10. Have a second person review the POA for clarity and completeness.
11. Prepare supporting documents (invoices, test reports, photos).
12. Do NOT submit yet if the deadline allows more time. Quality beats speed.

### Days 2-7: Appeal Submission

13. Finalize and submit the Plan of Action.
14. Activate backup selling channels if available.
15. Communicate with customers through alternative channels if needed.
16. Monitor appeal status daily.
17. Prepare escalation contacts in case of rejection.

### Days 7-30: Follow-Up

18. If appeal denied: analyze feedback, revise POA, resubmit.
19. If no response: escalate through appropriate channels.
20. Evaluate inventory disposition options (removal, liquidation).
21. Document the entire incident for post-mortem review.

## 6. Plan of Action (POA) Writing Guide

A Plan of Action is the formal document you submit to appeal a suspension.
The quality of your POA is the single biggest factor in reinstatement success.

### Structure

Every effective POA has three sections:

#### Section 1: Root Cause Analysis

What happened and why. Be specific, factual, and honest.

**Do:**
- Identify the specific root cause with concrete details
- Take responsibility where appropriate
- Show you understand the policy that was violated
- Reference specific ASINs, dates, and order IDs

**Do NOT:**
- Blame the platform, customers, or competitors
- Be vague ("We had some quality issues")
- Deny the issue if evidence contradicts you
- Write emotional appeals ("We are a small family business...")

**Example:**
"Between January 15 and February 2, 2026, 14 units of ASIN B0XXXXXXXX
(Widget Pro 2.0) were shipped with a manufacturing defect in the power
connector. Our pre-shipment inspection protocol failed to catch this defect
because the batch inspection was performed on a 2% sample rate, which was
insufficient for this production run from a new assembly line at our
supplier's facility."

#### Section 2: Corrective Actions Already Taken

What you have already done to fix the immediate problem.

**Do:**
- List specific, concrete actions with dates
- Show evidence of completion (attach documents)
- Address the root cause, not just the symptoms

**Example:**
"1. Recalled all remaining units from FBA inventory (removal order
   #XXXXXXXX submitted February 3, 2026).
2. Contacted all 14 affected customers, offered full refund and
   replacement at no cost (completion date: February 4, 2026).
3. Terminated the defective assembly line production and directed all
   new production to the verified assembly line.
4. Increased pre-shipment inspection sample rate from 2% to 8% for all
   future batches."

#### Section 3: Preventive Measures

What you will do to ensure this never happens again.

**Do:**
- Describe systemic changes, not one-time fixes
- Include monitoring mechanisms to catch future issues early
- Set measurable standards and review dates
- Demonstrate understanding of the platform's standards

**Example:**
"1. Implemented mandatory 8% AQL inspection for all production batches,
   with 100% inspection for first batch from any new production line.
2. Added final functional test for power connector on 100% of units
   before packaging.
3. Engaged third-party QC agency (SGS) for quarterly supplier audits.
4. Established weekly review of customer feedback for early detection of
   product quality issues, with escalation protocol when negative
   feedback rate exceeds 1% on any SKU."

### POA Formatting Rules

- Keep it concise: 1-2 pages maximum
- Use numbered lists, not long paragraphs
- No attachments in the body; reference them by name
- Professional tone throughout
- No marketing language or self-promotion
- Proofread for grammar and spelling
- Submit in the language preferred by the platform support team

### Common POA Mistakes That Lead to Rejection

1. Generic POA not specific to the actual violation
2. Denying the issue or blaming external parties
3. Corrective actions that do not address the root cause
4. No preventive measures (only reactive fixes)
5. Emotional language instead of factual analysis
6. Submitting before fully understanding the suspension reason
7. Copy-pasting templates without customization
8. Missing supporting documents referenced in the POA

## 7. Reinstatement Process by Platform

### Amazon

**Appeal Submission Path:**
1. Performance Notifications in Seller Central
2. Click "Appeal" on the specific notification
3. Submit POA with supporting documents
4. Response time: typically 2-7 business days

**Escalation Path (if denied):**
1. Revise POA addressing specific feedback in denial
2. Resubmit through Performance Notifications
3. If denied twice: email seller-performance@amazon.com
4. Executive escalation: jeff@amazon.com (routes to executive support team)
5. Legal escalation: engage attorney specializing in Amazon seller matters

**Tips:**
- Amazon support phone agents cannot lift performance suspensions
- Each resubmission resets the review queue; do not resubmit prematurely
- Attach invoices from the last 365 days for authenticity cases
- For IP complaints: contact the rights holder directly for retraction

### Shopee

**Appeal Submission Path:**
1. Seller Centre > Account Health > View violation details
2. Submit appeal through the provided form
3. Attach supporting evidence
4. Response time: typically 3-5 business days

**Escalation Path:**
1. Resubmit appeal with additional evidence
2. Contact Seller Support via chat or ticket
3. Request escalation to the Trust & Safety team
4. Regional seller support contacts (vary by market)

**Tips:**
- Penalty points may be reversed individually
- Some violations allow immediate correction without formal appeal
- Chat response rate issues can often be resolved by activating auto-reply
- Local seller communities often share market-specific escalation contacts

### TikTok Shop

**Appeal Submission Path:**
1. Seller Center > Violation Management > Appeal
2. Submit evidence and explanation
3. Response time: typically 3-7 business days

**Escalation Path:**
1. Resubmit with revised evidence
2. Contact Seller Support through Seller Center
3. Request escalation to compliance team
4. For content-related suspensions: appeal through the content review system

**Tips:**
- Content violations and shop violations may have separate appeal processes
- Product certification documents are frequently requested
- Video evidence (factory tours, product demonstrations) can strengthen appeals
- TikTok Shop policies evolve rapidly; verify current requirements before appeal

## 8. Multi-Account Considerations and Risks

### When Multiple Accounts Are Justified

Legitimate business reasons for operating multiple seller accounts:

- Separate legal entities (different companies with different ownership)
- Distinct product categories requiring separate branding
- Different geographic business units with independent operations
- Acquired businesses that came with existing seller accounts
- Manufacturer and retailer roles separated by business function

### Account Linking Detection Methods

Platforms actively detect linked accounts through:

| Signal | Detection Method |
|---|---|
| IP Address | Login from same IP across accounts |
| Browser Fingerprint | Same browser profile, cookies, extensions |
| Device ID | Same computer or phone used for multiple accounts |
| Bank Account | Same bank account linked to multiple accounts |
| Tax ID / Business License | Same entity documents across accounts |
| Address | Same registered or return address |
| Product Overlap | Identical or near-identical ASINs/listings |
| Behavioral Patterns | Similar login times, pricing patterns, response templates |
| Email Domain | Multiple accounts with emails from same domain |
| Phone Number | Same phone number or contact information |

### Risk Assessment for Multi-Account Operation

**High Risk (avoid):**
- Operating multiple accounts on same platform under same legal entity
  without platform approval
- Using VPN or browser profiles to mask account connections
- Sharing inventory or fulfillment infrastructure across linked accounts
  without disclosure

**Medium Risk (proceed with caution):**
- Separate legal entities on same platform with some shared personnel
- Different accounts in different marketplace regions (e.g., Amazon US
  vs Amazon EU under same entity -- generally permitted)
- Transitioning from one account to another during business restructuring

**Low Risk:**
- Completely separate businesses with no shared resources
- Platform-approved multi-store features (Shopee, TikTok)
- Different platforms entirely (Amazon + Shopee + TikTok under same entity)

## 9. Account Insurance and Backup Account Strategy

### Backup Account Strategy (Compliance-Focused)

The goal is not to circumvent platform rules but to ensure business continuity
through legitimate means.

**Legitimate Backup Approaches:**

1. **Multi-Platform Presence**
   - Maintain active accounts on Amazon, Shopee, TikTok Shop, and at least
     one DTC channel
   - Even if 80% of revenue comes from one platform, keep other accounts
     active with at least a subset of your catalog
   - This is the safest and most universally compliant backup strategy

2. **Separate Business Entities**
   - If your business structure justifies it, operate genuinely separate
     business entities with their own accounts
   - Each entity must have independent: legal registration, tax ID, bank
     account, physical address, internet connection, and management

3. **Authorized Distributor Relationships**
   - Establish relationships with authorized distributors who sell on
     platforms where you also sell
   - They carry your products under their accounts, providing continuity
     if your direct account is disrupted

4. **DTC Channel Development**
   - Build your own e-commerce presence (Shopify, WooCommerce, etc.)
   - Collect customer emails and build a direct communication channel
   - Even a small DTC channel provides critical backup during suspension

### Revenue Concentration Guidelines

| Concentration Level | Risk | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| > 90% on one account | Critical | Immediate diversification required |
| 70-90% on one account | High | Active diversification plan with timeline |
| 50-70% on one account | Medium | Ongoing diversification effort |
| < 50% on one account | Acceptable | Maintain and monitor |

### Account Data Backup Protocol

Regularly export and back up the following data from each account:

- [ ] Product catalog (all listings, images, descriptions)
- [ ] Customer order history
- [ ] Financial reports and transaction records
- [ ] Advertising campaign data and history
- [ ] Review and feedback records
- [ ] Supplier invoices and authorization documents
- [ ] Performance metrics history
- [ ] Correspondence with platform support

Backup frequency: weekly for active data, monthly for archives.
Storage: encrypted cloud storage, separate from seller account credentials.

## 10. Case Studies: Common Suspension Scenarios and Recovery

### Case Study 1: IP Complaint Suspension (Amazon)

**Scenario:** A seller of phone accessories receives three IP complaints in
30 days from a competitor claiming design patent infringement. Amazon suspends
the affected listings and issues an account-level warning.

**Root Cause:** The competitor holds a design patent on a specific phone case
shape. The seller's product has a similar but not identical design. The seller
had not performed a freedom-to-operate patent search before sourcing.

**Recovery Steps:**
1. Engaged a patent attorney to evaluate the design patent claims
2. Attorney issued opinion letter that the products do not infringe
3. Filed counter-notice with Amazon providing attorney opinion
4. Contacted the complainant's attorney to negotiate withdrawal
5. Complainant withdrew 2 of 3 claims; third was resolved via Amazon
   internal review
6. Listings reinstated after 18 days

**Lesson Learned:** Perform patent search before launching products in
competitive categories. Budget $500-1,500 for a freedom-to-operate opinion
on high-risk product designs.

**Preventive Measure Implemented:** Added patent search step to product
selection workflow for all new products in accessories categories.

### Case Study 2: Performance Metric Suspension (Shopee)

**Scenario:** A seller's late shipment rate spikes to 15% during a flash
sale event due to warehouse staffing shortage. Shopee issues penalty points
pushing the account into suspension range.

**Root Cause:** The seller ran a flash sale promotion without scaling
warehouse staffing. Order volume tripled but packing capacity remained flat,
causing 3-day shipping delays on 200+ orders.

**Recovery Steps:**
1. Immediately hired temporary warehouse staff
2. Extended work hours to clear backlog within 48 hours
3. Submitted appeal showing all delayed orders were now shipped and delivered
4. Provided evidence of staffing plan for future promotions
5. Penalty points reduced; account restored after 5 days

**Lesson Learned:** Scale fulfillment capacity before running promotions,
not after orders arrive.

**Preventive Measure Implemented:** Promotion launch checklist now includes
warehouse capacity verification and temporary staffing pre-arrangement for
any promotion expected to increase volume by more than 50%.

### Case Study 3: Counterfeit Claim Suspension (Amazon)

**Scenario:** A seller of branded electronics receives an authenticity
complaint from the brand owner. Amazon suspends the listing and requests
invoices proving authenticity.

**Root Cause:** The seller sourced the products from an unauthorized
distributor. The products were genuine but the supply chain was not
authorized by the brand, and the invoices were from a middleman rather
than an authorized distributor.

**Recovery Steps:**
1. Contacted the brand owner to establish authorization (denied)
2. Submitted invoices from the distributor (rejected by Amazon as
   insufficient)
3. Obtained a letter from the distributor confirming the product source
4. Engaged an Amazon suspension specialist for POA guidance
5. Revised POA committed to only sourcing from brand-authorized channels
6. Listing reinstated after 25 days, but seller chose to exit the brand
   and source own-brand alternatives

**Lesson Learned:** "Genuine product" is not enough; Amazon requires
authorized supply chain documentation. If you cannot obtain authorization
from the brand, the risk of selling that brand is too high.

**Preventive Measure Implemented:** New sourcing policy requires written
authorization from brand owners for all branded products before first order.
Shifted catalog strategy toward own-brand products with brand registry.

### Case Study 4: Review Manipulation Warning (Amazon)

**Scenario:** A seller includes product insert cards offering a "free gift"
in exchange for a "positive review." Amazon flags this as review manipulation
and issues a warning with listing suppression on the affected ASIN.

**Root Cause:** The insert card language was ambiguous. It said "Leave us a
5-star review and email us for a free gift." This directly conditions a reward
on a positive review, violating Amazon's review policy.

**Recovery Steps:**
1. Immediately halted all shipments containing the offending insert card
2. Redesigned insert card to comply with policy (no mention of reviews,
   only a thank-you and customer service contact)
3. Submitted POA showing old and new insert card designs
4. Provided timeline for when all old insert cards would be purged from
   FBA inventory
5. Warning resolved and listing restored after 7 days

**Lesson Learned:** Any insert card language that connects a benefit to a
review (positive or negative) violates policy. Even "honest review" language
with an attached incentive is risky.

**Preventive Measure Implemented:** All insert card designs now go through
a compliance review checklist before printing. Insert cards only contain:
brand story, customer service contact, and warranty information. No mention
of reviews in any form.

### Case Study 5: TikTok Shop Content Violation

**Scenario:** A seller's live stream is flagged for making unsubstantiated
health claims about a dietary supplement. TikTok Shop suspends the product
listing and issues a shop-level warning.

**Root Cause:** The live stream host made claims like "this will cure your
insomnia" and "clinically proven to reduce anxiety" without any clinical
evidence or FDA-approved labeling to support these claims.

**Recovery Steps:**
1. Removed all recorded content containing unsubstantiated claims
2. Submitted product certificates and lab reports showing compliant labeling
3. Provided a revised live stream script with only label-approved language
4. Completed TikTok Shop's seller education module on health product claims
5. Listing reinstated after 10 days with a content compliance plan

**Lesson Learned:** Live stream content is subject to the same advertising
claim standards as written listings. Train all live stream hosts on compliant
language before going live.

**Preventive Measure Implemented:** Created approved claim sheet for each
health-related product. All live stream hosts must review and sign off on
approved language before each session. Random live stream audits conducted
weekly by compliance team.
