## 1. Supply Chain Risk Categories

Every supply chain contains five categories of risk. Assess each category
for every product line and document the current risk level.

### 1.1 Supplier Risk

The risk that a supplier fails to deliver as agreed.

| Risk Factor | Description | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Financial instability | Supplier faces cash flow problems or bankruptcy | Late deliveries, quality cuts, requests for advance payment |
| Capacity constraints | Supplier cannot meet demand volume | Extended lead times, partial shipments, declined POs |
| Key person dependency | Supplier operations depend on one individual | All communication through one contact, no backup contacts |
| Labor issues | Strikes, labor shortages, or regulatory violations | Reports of labor disputes, high worker turnover |
| Technology failure | Equipment breakdown or IT system failure | Inconsistent production quality, missed deadlines |
| Ethical / compliance | Supplier violates labor, environmental, or safety laws | Audit failures, negative press, missing certifications |

### 1.2 Logistics Risk

The risk that goods do not move from origin to destination as planned.

| Risk Factor | Description | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Port congestion | Departure or arrival ports experience delays | Industry reports of vessel queuing, schedule changes |
| Carrier default | Shipping line or freight forwarder fails | Booking cancellations, financial distress reports |
| Customs delay | Shipment held for inspection or documentation issues | Increasing inspection rates, regulatory changes |
| Route disruption | Shipping lane blocked or rerouted | Geopolitical events, canal closures, weather |
| Last-mile failure | Final delivery carrier experiences systemic issues | Rising delivery failure rates, customer complaints |
| Cost volatility | Freight rates spike unexpectedly | Spot rate increases, surcharge announcements |

### 1.3 Quality Risk

The risk that products fail to meet specifications or safety standards.

| Risk Factor | Description | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing defect | Systematic quality failure in production | Increasing defect rate, customer returns spike |
| Material substitution | Supplier uses cheaper materials without disclosure | Weight changes, texture differences, test failures |
| Specification drift | Product gradually deviates from original spec | Subtle changes in dimensions, color, packaging |
| Contamination | Foreign substances or materials in product | Customer reports, lab test failures |
| Labeling error | Incorrect labels, certifications, or markings | Regulatory complaints, customs rejections |
| Counterfeit components | Sub-components sourced from unauthorized channels | Component failures, inconsistent performance |

### 1.4 Geopolitical Risk

The risk that political or regulatory changes disrupt supply or trade.

| Risk Factor | Description | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Tariff increase | Import duties raised on product category | Government trade policy announcements |
| Trade sanctions | Country or entity placed on sanctions list | Government advisories, industry alerts |
| Export restrictions | Source country restricts export of materials/goods | Regulatory changes, license requirements |
| Political instability | Civil unrest, government change in source country | News reports, travel advisories |
| Regulatory change | New product regulations in destination market | Federal register notices, industry body alerts |
| Currency controls | Source country implements capital or FX restrictions | Banking advisories, payment delays |

### 1.5 Natural Disaster Risk

The risk that environmental events disrupt production or logistics.

| Risk Factor | Description | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Flooding | Factory or logistics hub affected by floods | Seasonal patterns, geographic vulnerability |
| Earthquake | Seismic event damages production facilities | Proximity to fault lines, historical frequency |
| Typhoon / Hurricane | Storm disrupts shipping or production | Seasonal patterns, weather forecasting |
| Pandemic / Epidemic | Disease outbreak causes factory shutdowns or port closures | Public health advisories, early outbreak reports |
| Drought / Water shortage | Water-dependent production affected | Regional water supply reports |
| Power outage | Prolonged power disruption at factory or port | Grid reliability reports, seasonal demand patterns |

## 2. Supplier Diversification Framework

### The Tiered Supplier Model

Maintain three tiers of suppliers for every product contributing more than
15% of revenue.

#### Tier 1: Primary Supplier

- **Volume share**: 60-70% of total production
- **Relationship**: Deep, contractual, ongoing
- **Requirements**:
  - Formal supply agreement with SLA terms
  - Annual on-site audit completed
  - Quality management system documented (ISO 9001 or equivalent)
  - Product liability insurance verified
  - Financial stability assessed (Dun & Bradstreet or equivalent)
  - Minimum 2 contact persons at the factory
  - Backup production line or facility identified
- **Review cadence**: Quarterly performance review

#### Tier 2: Secondary Supplier

- **Volume share**: 20-30% of total production
- **Relationship**: Active, regular orders to maintain readiness
- **Requirements**:
  - Sample approved and on file (refreshed every 6 months)
  - MOQ and pricing negotiated and documented
  - Lead time verified with at least one production order
  - Basic quality audit completed
  - Can scale to 80% of primary supplier volume within 30 days
- **Review cadence**: Semi-annual performance review

#### Tier 3: Emergency Supplier

- **Volume share**: 0% (standby, no regular orders)
- **Relationship**: Qualified but dormant
- **Requirements**:
  - Sample approved and on file (refreshed annually)
  - Pricing and MOQ agreed in principle
  - Contact information current and verified quarterly
  - Can produce first order within 14-21 days of activation
  - No contractual commitment required
- **Review cadence**: Annual qualification check

### Supplier Qualification Scoring Matrix

Use this matrix to evaluate and compare potential suppliers across tiers.

| Criteria | Weight | Score 1-5 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product quality (sample evaluation) | 25% | | |
| Price competitiveness | 20% | | |
| Production capacity and scalability | 15% | | |
| Lead time reliability | 15% | | |
| Communication responsiveness | 10% | | |
| Financial stability | 5% | | |
| Certifications and compliance | 5% | | |
| Geographic risk profile | 5% | | |
| **Weighted Total** | **100%** | | |

Minimum score for qualification:
- Tier 1: 4.0 weighted average
- Tier 2: 3.5 weighted average
- Tier 3: 3.0 weighted average

### Supplier Relationship Management Calendar

| Activity | Frequency | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Order placement | Ongoing | Yes | Regular | No |
| Performance review meeting | Quarterly | Yes | No | No |
| On-site audit | Annual | Yes | No | No |

## 3. Geographic Diversification

### Why Geographic Diversification Matters

Concentrating all sourcing in one country exposes the business to correlated
risks: a single government policy change, natural disaster, or geopolitical
event can shut down the entire supply chain simultaneously. Geographic
diversification ensures that a disruption in one region does not halt all
production.

### Sourcing Region Profiles

#### China

- **Strengths**: Largest manufacturing base, widest product variety, mature
  export infrastructure, competitive pricing on most categories, fast
  prototyping and iteration
- **Risks**: US-China trade tensions and tariffs, rising labor costs,
  COVID-related disruption precedent, IP enforcement challenges
- **Key regions**: Shenzhen (electronics), Yiwu (small commodities),
  Dongguan (hardware, accessories), Guangzhou (textiles, garments),
  Ningbo (industrial goods)
- **Best for**: Electronics, accessories, home goods, general merchandise,
  complex manufactured products

#### Vietnam

- **Strengths**: Lower labor costs than China, growing manufacturing base,
  favorable trade agreements (CPTPP, EU-Vietnam FTA), young workforce
- **Risks**: Less mature infrastructure, limited supplier variety in some
  categories, quality consistency challenges, power supply constraints
- **Key regions**: Ho Chi Minh City (garments, footwear), Hanoi (electronics
  assembly), Binh Duong (furniture, woodworking)
- **Best for**: Textiles, footwear, furniture, basic electronics assembly,
  food products

#### India

- **Strengths**: Large labor pool, competitive pricing, strong in textiles
  and IT, growing manufacturing policy support (Make in India)
- **Risks**: Infrastructure gaps, bureaucratic complexity, quality
  consistency variance, longer lead times for some categories
- **Key regions**: Mumbai/Pune (engineering), Tirupur (textiles),
  Delhi/NCR (handicrafts, leather), Bangalore (IT hardware)
- **Best for**: Textiles, leather goods, handicrafts, jewelry, generic
  pharmaceuticals, IT services

#### Turkey

- **Strengths**: Proximity to Europe and Middle East, strong textile
  tradition, competitive in home goods, short transit times to EU
- **Risks**: Currency volatility (TRY), political uncertainty, customs
  complexity, smaller manufacturing base than China
- **Key regions**: Istanbul (textiles, general), Bursa (automotive parts),
  Denizli (home textiles), Gaziantep (carpets)
- **Best for**: Textiles, home textiles, furniture, food products,
  EU-bound goods

#### Mexico

- **Strengths**: USMCA trade benefits for US market, proximity to US
  (short transit times), growing manufacturing sector, nearshoring trend
- **Risks**: Security concerns in some regions, limited product category
  breadth, higher labor costs than Asia, infrastructure variance
- **Key regions**: Monterrey (manufacturing), Guadalajara (electronics),
  Mexico City (general), Queretaro (automotive)
- **Best for**: US-market goods benefiting from USMCA, automotive parts,
  medical devices, food and beverage

#### Thailand and Indonesia

- **Strengths**: Competitive labor costs, natural resources, growing export
  infrastructure, ASEAN trade agreements
- **Risks**: Political instability (varies), infrastructure outside major
  cities, language barriers, flooding risk (Thailand)
- **Key regions**: Bangkok (Thailand, general), Eastern Seaboard (Thailand,
  electronics), Jakarta (Indonesia, general), Surabaya (Indonesia,
  manufacturing)
- **Best for**: Food products, natural cosmetics, rubber goods, electronics
  assembly, wooden products

### Geographic Diversification Decision Framework

| Current State | Risk Level | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 100% from one country | Critical | Immediately qualify Tier 2 supplier in different country |
| 80%+ from one country | High | Qualify Tier 2 in different country within 6 months |
| 60-80% from one country | Medium | Qualify Tier 3 in different country within 12 months |
| < 60% from one country | Acceptable | Maintain and review annually |

### Transition Planning

When diversifying to a new region, follow this phased approach:

1. **Research phase (Month 1-2)**: Identify 5-10 potential suppliers through
   trade shows, B2B platforms (Alibaba, Global Sources, IndiaMART), and
   industry referrals.

2. **Sample phase (Month 2-3)**: Request samples from top 3-5 candidates.
   Evaluate against current product spec. Test for quality, materials, and
   compliance.

3. **Trial order phase (Month 3-5)**: Place small trial order (1-2x MOQ)
   with top 2 candidates. Evaluate full production quality, packaging,
   lead time accuracy, and communication.

4. **Qualification phase (Month 5-6)**: Conduct remote or on-site audit of
   the winning candidate. Verify certifications, capacity, and financial
   stability. Assign tier status.

5. **Integration phase (Month 6-9)**: Gradually shift volume to the new
   supplier. Start at 10-20% of category volume, increase based on
   performance. Maintain primary supplier relationship.

## 4. Quality Risk Mitigation

### Inspection Protocol Framework

#### Pre-Production Inspection (PPI)

**When**: Before production begins on a new order
**Purpose**: Verify raw materials, components, and production readiness
**Scope**:
- Raw material quality and specification match
- Component inventory for the order
- Production line setup and tooling
- Color and material samples vs. approved standards
- Packaging materials available and correct

**Decision gate**: Production proceeds only after PPI passes.

#### During Production Inspection (DPI)

**When**: When 20-30% of production is complete
**Purpose**: Catch systemic defects early before full batch is produced
**Scope**:
- Workmanship quality on completed units
- Dimensional accuracy vs. specification
- Functional testing on sample units
- Production speed and capacity on track
- Identify any deviation from approved sample

**Decision gate**: Continue, adjust, or halt production.

#### Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI)

**When**: When 80-100% of production is complete and packed
**Purpose**: Final quality verification before shipment
**Scope**:
- AQL sampling per agreed standard (typically AQL 2.5 for major defects,
  AQL 4.0 for minor defects)
- Visual inspection, measurement, and functional testing
- Packaging and labeling verification
- Barcode and UPC scanning
- Carton weight and dimensions
- Drop test on packed cartons (if applicable)
- Certificate and documentation review

**Decision gate**: Ship, rework, or reject.

#### Container Loading Inspection (CLI)

**When**: During container loading at the factory or warehouse
**Purpose**: Verify correct quantity loaded and proper handling
**Scope**:
- Container condition (clean, dry, no odor, no damage)
- Carton count matches packing list
- Loading arrangement (heavy on bottom, fragile protected)
- Container seal number recorded and matched to documentation
- Photos of loaded container before sealing

### Acceptable Quality Level (AQL) Standards

| Defect Severity | AQL Level | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 0 | Defect that could harm the end user (safety hazard) |
| Major | 2.5 | Defect that makes the product unfit for intended use |
| Minor | 4.0 | Defect that does not affect function but reduces perceived quality |

**Sample size by order quantity (AQL General Inspection Level II):**

| Order Quantity | Sample Size | Accept (Major 2.5) | Reject (Major 2.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 - 8 | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| 9 - 15 | 3 | 0 | 1 |
| 16 - 25 | 5 | 0 | 1 |
| 26 - 50 | 8 | 0 | 1 |
| 51 - 90 | 13 | 1 | 2 |
| 91 - 150 | 20 | 1 | 2 |
| 151 - 280 | 32 | 2 | 3 |
| 281 - 500 | 50 | 3 | 4 |
| 501 - 1,200 | 80 | 5 | 6 |
| 1,201 - 3,200 | 125 | 7 | 8 |
| 3,201 - 10,000 | 200 | 10 | 11 |
| 10,001 - 35,000 | 315 | 14 | 15 |

### Third-Party QC Agencies

For sellers who cannot conduct inspections in person, third-party QC
agencies provide professional inspection services.

**Major agencies:**
- SGS (global, largest inspection company)
- Bureau Veritas (global, strong in consumer products)
- Intertek (global, strong in textiles and electronics)
- TUV (global, strong in safety and compliance testing)
- QIMA (Asia-focused, competitive pricing, online booking)
- V-Trust (China-focused, competitive pricing)

**Cost guidance:**
- Single inspection (1 inspector, 1 day): $250-400 USD
- Factory audit (1-2 days): $400-800 USD
- Lab testing: varies by test ($100-2,000+ per test)

**When to use third-party QC:**
- First order from any new supplier
- Orders exceeding $10,000 in value
- Products with safety or compliance requirements
- Any supplier with a history of quality issues
- Complex products with multiple components

| Market | Key Regulations | Common Tests Required |
|---|---|---|
| USA | CPSC, FDA, FCC, EPA | CPSIA (children's), FCC (electronics), FDA (food/cosmetics) |
| EU | CE marking, REACH, RoHS | CE conformity, REACH compliance, RoHS (electronics) |
| UK | UKCA marking, UK REACH | UKCA (post-Brexit CE equivalent), UK REACH |
| Australia | ACCC, RCM | SAA (electrical), RCM (telecom), ACCC safety |
| Japan | PSE, TELEC, METI | PSE (electrical), TELEC (wireless), JIS standards |

## 5. Inventory Buffer Strategy

### Safety Stock Calculation

Safety stock protects against supply disruption. Calculate for each SKU
based on disruption risk.

**Basic formula:**

```
Safety Stock = (Maximum Lead Time - Average Lead Time) x Average Daily Sales
```

**Risk-adjusted formula:**

```
Safety Stock = Z x SQRT(Lead Time) x Demand Standard Deviation
```

Where Z is the service level factor:
- 90% service level: Z = 1.28
- 95% service level: Z = 1.65
- 99% service level: Z = 2.33

### Buffer Levels by Risk Profile

| Risk Profile | Safety Stock Target | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| Low risk | 14 days of sales | Stable supplier, reliable logistics, commodity product |
| Medium risk | 30 days of sales | Single supplier, some logistics variability |
| High risk | 45-60 days of sales | Single supplier in high-risk region, seasonal product |
| Critical risk | 60-90 days of sales | Single source, long lead time, no substitute available |

### Inventory Distribution Strategy

Do not store all inventory in one location. Distribute across:

- **Origin warehouse** (China / source country): Pre-inspected stock ready
  to ship on demand
- **Destination FBA / fulfillment center**: Stock positioned for fast delivery
- **3PL backup warehouse**: Overflow and reserve stock in destination country
- **In-transit buffer**: Maintain overlapping shipments so new stock arrives
  before current stock depletes

### Disruption Scenario Planning

For each product line, calculate these scenarios:

| Scenario | Assumption | Runway | Action Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Lead time on schedule | Full stock | Standard reorder point |
| Minor delay | Lead time +7 days | -7 days of safety stock | Notify supplier, monitor |
| Major delay | Lead time +21 days | Potential stockout | Activate Tier 2 supplier |
| Supplier failure | No delivery for 45+ days | Stockout likely | Activate Tier 3, reduce ad spend |
| Regional disruption | All suppliers in region offline | Depends on buffer | Activate out-of-region supplier |

### Reorder Point Formula

```
Reorder Point = (Average Daily Sales x Lead Time in Days) + Safety Stock
```

Review and recalculate reorder points:
- Monthly for fast-moving SKUs (top 20% by velocity)
- Quarterly for standard SKUs
- After any disruption event that affects lead time assumptions

## 6. Geopolitical Risk Assessment

### Trade War and Tariff Monitoring

#### Current High-Risk Trade Corridors

Monitor these corridors for tariff and policy changes:

| Corridor | Current Risk | Key Factors |
|---|---|---|
| China to USA | High | Section 301 tariffs, evolving exclusion lists |
| China to EU | Medium | Anti-dumping duties on select categories |
| China to India | High | Import restrictions, BIS licensing |
| Russia to EU/US | Critical | Broad sanctions regime |
| Southeast Asia to USA | Medium | Circumvention investigations (transshipment) |

#### Tariff Impact Assessment

For each product line, calculate the tariff sensitivity:

```
Tariff Impact = (Current Duty Rate x Landed Cost) / Selling Price
```

| Tariff Impact | Risk Level | Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 5% of selling price | Low | Monitor, absorb in margin |
| 5-15% of selling price | Medium | Evaluate sourcing alternatives |
| > 15% of selling price | High | Diversify sourcing immediately |

### Sanctions and Export Controls

**Before sourcing from any new country or supplier, verify:**

- [ ] Country is not subject to comprehensive sanctions (OFAC, EU sanctions)
- [ ] Supplier entity is not on any restricted party list (SDN, Entity List)
- [ ] Product is not subject to export controls (EAR, dual-use regulations)
- [ ] Payment routing does not involve sanctioned financial institutions
- [ ] End-use and end-user are compliant with regulations

**Screening tools:**
- US: OFAC SDN list, BIS Entity List, Denied Persons List
- EU: EU Sanctions Map, Consolidated Financial Sanctions List
- UK: OFSI Consolidated List
- Commercial: Dow Jones, LexisNexis, Descartes Visual Compliance

### Regulatory Change Monitoring

Subscribe to these information sources for early warning:

- Federal Register (US regulatory changes)
- Official Journal of the EU (EU regulatory changes)
- CBP CSMS (US Customs and Border Protection trade alerts)
- USTR announcements (trade policy changes)
- Industry trade associations (category-specific alerts)
- Freight forwarder advisories (logistics and customs changes)
- Legal counsel newsletters (trade law updates)

### Geopolitical Risk Response Framework

| Alert Level | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | No active threats to supply corridor | Standard monitoring |
| Yellow | Proposed tariff/regulation change | Assess impact, prepare contingency |
| Orange | Enacted change with implementation timeline | Activate diversification plan |
| Red | Immediate disruption (sanctions, trade ban) | Execute emergency sourcing protocol |

## 7. Natural Disaster Preparedness

### Factory Region Risk Profiles

#### High-Risk Regions by Disaster Type

| Region | Primary Risks | Peak Risk Season |
|---|---|---|
| Guangdong, China | Typhoon, flooding | June - September |
| Yangtze Delta, China | Flooding, typhoon | June - August |
| Sichuan, China | Earthquake | Year-round |
| Central Vietnam | Typhoon, flooding | September - November |
| Chennai, India | Cyclone, flooding | October - December |
| Bangkok, Thailand | Flooding | August - November |
| Java, Indonesia | Earthquake, volcanic | Year-round |
| Izmir/Istanbul, Turkey | Earthquake | Year-round |
| Nuevo Leon, Mexico | Hurricane, flooding | June - November |

### Pre-Disaster Preparation

For every factory in a high-risk region:

1. **Know your factory's specific location and elevation.** Factories in
   flood plains or coastal zones face higher risk than those on higher ground.

2. **Request the factory's disaster preparedness plan.** Reputable factories
   have documented plans. If they do not, this is a risk factor.

3. **Verify the factory has insurance.** Ask for evidence of property and
   business interruption insurance.

4. **Map your critical path.** Know which components come from the at-risk
   region and what the alternative sourcing path is.

5. **Build seasonal buffers.** Increase safety stock by 2-4 weeks before
   peak disaster seasons in your source regions.

### Disaster Response Protocol

**Within 24 hours of a disaster event:**

1. Contact the supplier directly to assess status.
   - Is the factory damaged?
   - Are workers safe and able to return to work?
   - What is the estimated recovery timeline?
   - Are materials and in-process inventory damaged?

2. Contact the freight forwarder.
   - Are ports and logistics routes affected?
   - What is the estimated delay?
   - Are alternative routes available?

3. Assess inventory position.
   - Calculate days of stock remaining at current velocity.
   - Identify which SKUs are most at risk of stockout.

**Within 48-72 hours:**

4. Activate Tier 2 supplier if primary factory recovery exceeds 14 days.
5. Reduce advertising spend on at-risk SKUs to extend runway.
6. Communicate proactively with customers if fulfillment delays are expected.
7. Update platform fulfillment promises to avoid late shipment penalties.

**Within 1-2 weeks:**

8. Place orders with Tier 2 or Tier 3 suppliers to cover the gap.
9. Evaluate whether air freight is justified for critical inventory.
10. Begin longer-term assessment of whether to permanently shift volume.

### Insurance Coverage for Natural Disaster Risk

| Insurance Type | What It Covers | When to Purchase |
|---|---|---|
| Cargo / marine insurance | Goods damaged or lost in transit | Every shipment |
| Business interruption | Revenue loss from supply disruption | If revenue exceeds $500K/year |
| Contingent business interruption | Loss caused by supplier's disruption | If single-supplier dependent |
| Trade disruption insurance | Covers broader supply chain events | High-value, complex supply chains |

## 8. Communication Protocol During Disruptions

### Internal Communication

#### Disruption Alert Template

```
SUPPLY CHAIN DISRUPTION ALERT

Date:          [Date]
Severity:      [Low / Medium / High / Critical]
Category:      [Supplier / Logistics / Quality / Geopolitical / Natural Disaster]

SUMMARY:
[One paragraph describing what happened]

AFFECTED PRODUCTS:
- [SKU 1] - [Impact description]
- [SKU 2] - [Impact description]

CURRENT INVENTORY POSITION:
- [SKU 1]: [X] days of stock remaining at current velocity
- [SKU 2]: [X] days of stock remaining at current velocity

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS TAKEN:
1. [Action 1]
2. [Action 2]

NEXT STEPS:
1. [Next step 1] - Owner: [Name] - Deadline: [Date]
2. [Next step 2] - Owner: [Name] - Deadline: [Date]

ESTIMATED RESOLUTION:
[Date or date range]
```

### Supplier Communication

#### Initial Outreach Template

```
Subject: Urgent - Order Status Inquiry [PO Number]

Dear [Supplier Contact],

We are writing to inquire about the status of our current order(s) in
light of [describe the event: typhoon, factory fire, COVID lockdown, etc.].

Please provide an update on the following:

1. Current status of PO [number] (scheduled ship date: [date])
2. Is your factory operational? If not, when do you expect to resume?
3. Is our inventory (finished goods and raw materials) safe and undamaged?
4. What is your revised estimated ship date?

We understand this may be a difficult time and appreciate any information
you can share. Please reply at your earliest convenience.

Best regards,
[Your name]
```

### Customer Communication

#### Proactive Delay Notification (use only if fulfillment SLA is at risk)

If a disruption will cause delivery delays beyond platform promises, notify
customers proactively. This is preferable to late shipment penalties and
negative feedback.

Key principles:
- Be honest about the delay without over-sharing details
- Provide a revised timeline
- Offer options (wait, cancel, alternative product)
- Respond to follow-up inquiries within 24 hours

### Platform Communication

If a disruption affects your ability to meet platform SLAs:

1. **Amazon**: Use Vacation Mode or adjust handling time in Seller Central
   before SLA breaches occur. For FBA, no action needed on existing stock.
   For FBM, extend handling time to reflect realistic delivery timeline.

2. **Shopee**: Adjust shipping settings in Seller Centre. Contact seller
   support if systemic delay affects multiple orders.

3. **TikTok Shop**: Update fulfillment settings in Seller Center. Contact
   seller support for bulk order extensions if needed.

## 9. Alternative Sourcing Activation Checklist

When a primary supplier fails or a disruption requires activating backup
sourcing, follow this checklist.

### Phase 1: Decision (Day 0-1)

- [ ] Confirm the primary supplier disruption is real and sustained
  (not a temporary communication gap)
- [ ] Calculate remaining inventory runway for affected SKUs
- [ ] Determine severity: is this a temporary delay or a long-term failure?
- [ ] Decision: activate Tier 2, Tier 3, or both?
- [ ] Assign a single point of contact to manage the activation

### Phase 2: Supplier Contact (Day 1-2)

- [ ] Contact Tier 2 supplier with order requirements
- [ ] Confirm current pricing (may differ from last negotiation)
- [ ] Confirm current lead time for the specific order quantity
- [ ] Confirm quality standard: send latest approved sample photos and
  specification sheet
- [ ] Confirm packaging and labeling requirements (platform-specific labels,
  FNSKU, etc.)
- [ ] Request production timeline with milestone dates
- [ ] Issue purchase order

### Phase 3: Quality Assurance (During Production)

- [ ] Schedule DPI at 20-30% production completion
- [ ] Compare DPI results against approved standard
- [ ] Address any deviations before production continues
- [ ] Schedule PSI at 80-100% production completion
- [ ] Approve shipment only after PSI passes

### Phase 4: Logistics Coordination (Pre-Shipment)

- [ ] Confirm shipping method (sea, air, express) based on urgency
- [ ] Book freight with forwarder
- [ ] Prepare all customs documentation for new origin country (if
  geographic diversification)
- [ ] Verify HS codes and duty rates for new origin country
- [ ] Arrange cargo insurance for the shipment
- [ ] Coordinate receiving at destination warehouse

### Phase 5: Transition Management (Post-Delivery)

- [ ] Inspect received goods against quality standard
- [ ] Compare quality to primary supplier output
- [ ] Update inventory systems with new stock
- [ ] Monitor customer feedback on first batch from new supplier
- [ ] Document the activation: what worked, what was slow, what to improve
- [ ] Update supplier tier assessment based on activation performance
- [ ] Decide: maintain dual sourcing or return to primary supplier when
  available?
