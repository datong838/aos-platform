# Seasonal Planning: Prepare for Peak Sales Events and Holiday Campaigns

Skill type: Technique + Reference

Help sellers plan, execute, and analyze peak-season campaigns across global
e-commerce platforms. Seasonal planning coordinates inventory, advertising,
listing optimization, and customer service into a single timeline so that every
team moves in lockstep toward a shared launch date.

## Core Principle

> **Peak season success is determined 90 days before the event, not during it.
> By the time Black Friday arrives, your inventory is either in the warehouse or
> it is not. There are no last-minute saves.**

The sellers who win peak season are the ones who treat it as a logistics and
planning exercise, not a marketing exercise. Marketing amplifies what is already
in place -- it cannot compensate for out-of-stock products, unoptimized
listings, or ad campaigns that were set up the night before.

Most cross-border sellers lose money during peak seasons. They over-order
inventory they cannot sell through, launch ads without testing creative, or
miss shipping cutoffs because they did not account for factory closures. The
framework below converts seasonal chaos into a repeatable process.

## The Peak Season Timeline

Every peak event follows the same preparation arc regardless of platform or
market. The timeline below uses T-Day as the event start date and counts
backward.

### T-90 Days: Strategic Planning

This is the most important milestone. Decisions made here lock in 80% of your
peak-season outcome.

- **Review prior year data.** Pull last year's sales, ad spend, conversion
  rates, and inventory levels for the same event. Identify what sold, what
  stalled, and what stocked out.
- **Select hero SKUs.** Choose 3-5 products that will receive the bulk of
  promotional and advertising investment. These should be proven sellers with
  strong reviews (4.0+ stars, 50+ reviews minimum).
- **Place production orders.** Calculate required inventory using the seasonal
  demand multiplier (see Inventory Pre-positioning below). Submit POs to
  suppliers with confirmed delivery dates.
- **Set total budget.** Allocate budget across inventory procurement, freight,
  advertising, promotions, and contingency (10-15% reserve). Lock this number
  now; do not increase it under pressure later.

### T-60 Days: Logistics Execution

- **Ship inventory to destination warehouses.** For ocean freight from China to
  US/EU FBA, this is the absolute last date for sea shipment. Any later and you
  must air-freight at 5-8x the cost.
- **Confirm FBA receiving capacity.** Check restock limits in Seller Central. If
  limits are tight, ship earlier or split shipments across fulfillment centers.
- **Begin keyword research.** Identify seasonal search terms that spike during
  the event period. Tools: Helium 10 (Cerebro/Magnet), Amazon Brand Analytics,
  Google Trends.
- **Plan promotional pricing.** Calculate the deepest discount you can offer
  while maintaining a positive unit margin after advertising cost. For Amazon
  deals, submit Lightning Deal and Best Deal applications (Amazon requires 4-6
  week lead time).
- **Brief customer service team.** Share expected volume increase, common product
  questions, return policy for the event, and escalation procedures.

### T-45 Days: Content Preparation

- **Create seasonal listing variations.** Update main images, A+ content, and
  bullet points with seasonal messaging. Do not change the main keyword-rich
  title -- add seasonal terms to backend search fields or secondary bullet
  points.
- **Design brand store updates.** Build a seasonal landing page in your Amazon
  Brand Store (or Shopee/Lazada shop page) that highlights hero SKUs, bundles,
  and deals.
- **Prepare social media content.** Draft and schedule posts across Instagram,
  TikTok, Facebook, and any platform-specific social commerce channels (TikTok
  Shop, Shopee Live).
- **Build advertising campaigns (paused).** Set up all Sponsored Products,
  Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display campaigns. Target seasonal keywords.
  Set initial bids at 1.5x your normal CPC. Leave campaigns paused until
  activation day.

### T-30 Days: Early Activation

- **Launch early-access campaigns.** Run low-budget "teaser" ads to test
  creative, keywords, and targeting. Spend 20-30% of your normal daily budget
  to gather data before the main push.
- **Accelerate review velocity.** Use Amazon Vine (if eligible), follow-up
  email sequences, or product inserts to build review count before the event.
  More reviews = higher conversion during peak traffic.
- **Test all campaign mechanics.** Verify coupon codes work, deal pricing is
  correct, and landing page links resolve properly. A broken coupon during
  Black Friday costs thousands in lost sales.
- **Verify inventory arrival.** Confirm units are in "Available" status in FBA,
  not stuck in "Receiving" or "In Transit." If units are delayed, activate
  backup FBM fulfillment from a 3PL.

### T-14 Days: Budget Ramp

- **Activate all advertising campaigns.** Unpause every campaign that was built
  at T-45.
- **Ramp budgets to 2-3x normal levels.** Increase daily budgets gradually:
  1.5x in week one, 2-3x in week two. Sudden budget spikes can trigger Amazon's
  spend pacing algorithm to throttle delivery.
- **Final inventory check.** Calculate days of supply at the projected peak
  sell-through rate. If any hero SKU has fewer than 14 days of supply at peak
  rate, activate contingency (air freight, FBM backup, or price increase to
  slow demand).
- **Submit and activate promotional deals.** Ensure Lightning Deals, coupons,
  and price discounts are live and displaying correctly on the listing.

### T-Day: Peak Execution

- **Monitor hourly metrics.** Track sales, ad spend, ACoS, conversion rate,
  and inventory levels every 1-2 hours. Set up a shared dashboard (Google
  Sheets or a tool like Sellerboard) for real-time visibility.
- **Adjust bids every 4 hours.** During peak traffic, CPCs can spike 2-5x.
  Increase bids on high-converting campaigns and pause underperformers. Use
  dayparting if the platform supports it (Sponsored Products does not natively;
  use third-party tools).
- **Customer service on standby.** Have dedicated team members monitoring
  messages, reviews, and order issues. Response time during peak season directly
  affects account health metrics.
- **Track competitor activity.** Monitor competitor pricing, deal badges, and
  ad placements. If a direct competitor drops price below yours, decide whether
  to match or differentiate on value (bundles, gifting messaging, superior
  reviews).

### T+7 Days: Wind Down

- **Reduce ad budgets to normal.** Step down budgets gradually over 3-5 days
  rather than cutting immediately. Post-event traffic often converts at a
  reasonable rate as late shoppers complete purchases.
- **Analyze initial results.** Pull top-line metrics: total revenue, total ad
  spend, blended ACoS, units sold per SKU, conversion rate delta vs. normal
  period.
- **Begin returns preparation.** Peak season generates 15-30% higher return
  rates than normal periods. Ensure return processing capacity is available
  and customer service scripts address common return reasons.

### T+14 Days: Post-Mortem

- **Full performance analysis.** Complete the Post-season Analysis Framework
  (see below).
- **Document learnings.** Write a 1-page post-mortem for each hero SKU
  covering what worked, what failed, and specific changes for next time.
- **Update playbook.** Revise timelines, budget allocations, and keyword lists
  based on actual data. Store the updated playbook where the team can access
  it for the next event.
- **Inventory reconciliation.** Compare actual sell-through vs. forecast. If
  significant overstock remains, activate the liquidation plan immediately
  (do not wait -- storage fees start compounding).

## Inventory Pre-positioning Strategy

Peak-season inventory planning is the single highest-stakes decision in the
entire timeline. Getting it wrong in either direction is costly.

### How Much Extra to Order

Calculate peak inventory using the **seasonal demand multiplier:**

```
Peak Order Quantity = Normal Monthly Sales x Seasonal Multiplier x Event Duration (months) + Safety Stock
```

Typical seasonal multipliers by event:

| Event                | Multiplier vs. Normal Month | Notes                              |
|----------------------|-----------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Amazon Prime Day     | 1.5-2.5x                   | 2-day event, but halo lasts 1 week |
| Black Friday / BFCM  | 2.0-4.0x                   | 5-day window (BF through CM)       |
| 11.11 Singles' Day   | 3.0-6.0x                   | Biggest single-day event globally   |
| 12.12                | 1.5-2.5x                   | Smaller than 11.11 but significant  |
| Christmas season     | 1.5-3.0x                   | Sustained 3-4 week peak            |
| Chinese New Year     | 0.3-0.5x                   | Sales drop; plan for factory closure |

**First-time sellers:** Use the low end of the multiplier range. It is better to
stock out briefly than to hold 3 months of excess inventory post-peak.

**Experienced sellers with prior year data:** Calculate your actual year-over-year
multiplier and add a 10-20% buffer.

### When to Ship

Work backward from the T-Day and add lead time buffers:

```
Ship-by Date = T-Day - FBA Receiving Buffer - Transit Time - Customs - Domestic Freight
```

Example for Black Friday (late November), shipping from China to US FBA:
- FBA receiving buffer: 14 days (Q4 receiving is slow)
- Ocean transit: 25 days
- Customs clearance: 5 days
- China domestic freight: 3 days
- Total lead time: 47 days
- Ship-by date: Early October (T-50 minimum)

**Critical rule:** If you miss the ocean freight cutoff, do not panic-order via
air freight unless the SKU's gross margin can absorb the 5-8x shipping cost
increase. Air freighting a low-margin product for peak season is a common way
to turn a profitable Q4 into a loss.

## Advertising Budget Ramp Plan

Advertising during peak season follows a three-phase pattern: warm up, peak
spend, and cool down.

### Phase 1: Warm Up (T-30 to T-14)

- **Budget:** 20-30% of normal daily spend.
- **Goal:** Test creative, identify winning keywords, and build campaign
  history so Amazon's algorithm has data to optimize delivery.
- **Actions:** Run auto campaigns and broad-match manual campaigns. Harvest
  converting search terms. Pause non-converting keywords.

### Phase 2: Peak Spend (T-14 to T+3)

- **Budget:** 2-3x normal daily spend. For hero SKUs, consider 4-5x.
- **Goal:** Maximum visibility on high-converting keywords during peak traffic.
- **Actions:** Shift budget to exact-match campaigns on proven winners. Increase
  bids by 50-100% on top-performing keywords. Activate Sponsored Brands and
  Sponsored Display for brand defense and retargeting.
- **Bid strategy:** Use dynamic bids (up and down) during peak days. Amazon
  will automatically increase bids when conversion probability is high
  (typically top-of-search placements on event days).

### Phase 3: Cool Down (T+3 to T+14)

- **Budget:** Step down to 1.5x normal, then return to baseline by T+14.
- **Goal:** Capture late-funnel converters and post-event bargain hunters.
- **Actions:** Reduce bids to pre-event levels. Pause broad-match campaigns.
  Maintain exact-match on proven keywords at normal CPC.

### Budget Allocation by Campaign Type

| Campaign Type           | % of Peak Budget | Purpose                           |
|-------------------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------|
| Sponsored Products      | 60-70%          | Direct conversion on search       |
| Sponsored Brands        | 15-20%          | Brand visibility, Store traffic   |
| Sponsored Display       | 10-15%          | Retargeting, competitor conquest  |
| DSP (if applicable)     | 5-10%           | Off-Amazon audience building      |

### ACoS Expectations

Peak-season ACoS will be higher than normal because CPCs spike with
competition. Expect ACoS to increase 30-60% vs. normal periods. The tradeoff
is acceptable if total profit (higher volume x slightly lower margin) exceeds
non-peak profit. Evaluate TACoS (Total Advertising Cost of Sales) rather than
campaign-level ACoS during peak events.

## Listing Optimization for Seasonal Keywords

Seasonal keywords are search terms that spike dramatically around specific
events and then drop back to near-zero. Capturing this traffic requires
temporary listing optimization.

### Keyword Integration Strategy

- **Backend search terms:** Add seasonal keywords (e.g., "black friday deal,"
  "christmas gift for dad," "valentines day gift") to the backend search term
  fields. This captures traffic without cluttering your visible listing copy.
- **Bullet points:** Weave 1-2 seasonal phrases into existing bullets
  naturally. Example: "Perfect stocking stuffer for the holidays" or "Great
  Mother's Day gift idea."
- **A+ content:** Update one A+ module with seasonal imagery and copy. Keep
  the rest of your A+ content evergreen.
- **Do NOT change your title for seasonal keywords.** Title changes can trigger
  Amazon's listing review process, suppressing your listing during the exact
  window you need it active.

### Timing

- Add seasonal keywords at T-30 (they take 24-48 hours to index).
- Remove seasonal keywords at T+14 (stale seasonal terms hurt relevance).
- Revert A+ content and bullet points to evergreen versions by T+21.

### Seasonal Keyword Sources

1. Amazon Brand Analytics (Search Query Performance, top search terms by week)
2. Helium 10 Magnet / Cerebro (filter by seasonal trend)
3. Google Trends (compare year-over-year spikes for keyword timing)
4. Competitor listings (check what seasonal terms top competitors add)
5. Platform-specific trending searches (Shopee Trending, Lazada search suggest)

## Customer Service Capacity Planning

Peak seasons generate 2-4x normal message volume, higher return rates, and
more escalations. Under-staffing customer service during peak season destroys
account health metrics and review ratings.

### Staffing Model

```
Peak CS Headcount = (Normal Daily Messages x Peak Multiplier) / Messages per Agent per Day
```

Typical values:
- Normal daily messages: varies by catalog size (10-100+)
- Peak multiplier: 2-4x during BFCM week, 1.5-2x in surrounding weeks
- Messages per agent per day: 40-60 (including follow-ups)

### Preparation Checklist

1. **Hire or contract temporary CS staff 30 days before the event.** Training
   takes 1-2 weeks for product knowledge and platform policies.
2. **Create canned responses** for the 10 most common peak-season questions:
   shipping estimates, deal eligibility, gift wrapping, return window, tracking
   status, coupon issues, product compatibility, stock availability, order
   modification, and delivery guarantees.
3. **Set up escalation tiers.** Tier 1 handles standard inquiries (canned
   responses). Tier 2 handles refunds, replacements, and A-to-Z claims. Tier 3
   (owner/manager) handles negative reviews and account health threats.
4. **Pre-authorize refund thresholds.** Allow Tier 1 agents to issue refunds up
   to a set amount (e.g., $25) without escalation. This reduces response time
   during the highest-volume days.
5. **Monitor review velocity daily.** During peak season, a single 1-star
   review on a hero SKU can tank conversion. Have a process to respond to
   negative reviews within 4 hours (contact buyer, offer resolution, request
   updated review if issue is resolved).

## Post-season Analysis Framework

Run this analysis within 14 days of every peak event. The goal is to convert
raw data into actionable changes for the next event.

### Metrics to Pull

| Metric                        | Compare Against          | Target                       |
|-------------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| Total revenue                 | Prior year same event    | +15-30% YoY growth          |
| Units sold per hero SKU       | Forecast                 | Within 10% of forecast       |
| Blended ACoS                  | Normal period ACoS       | < 1.5x normal ACoS          |
| TACoS                         | Prior year same event    | Flat or declining            |
| Conversion rate               | Normal period CVR        | +20-50% during event         |
| Stockout days (per SKU)       | Zero                     | Zero                         |
| Return rate                   | Normal period returns    | < 2x normal rate             |
| Customer service response time | 24-hour SLA             | < 12 hours during peak       |
| Excess inventory post-event   | Zero                     | < 15% of peak order qty      |

### Per-SKU Analysis Questions

1. Did we hit the sales target? (traffic vs. conversion breakdown)
2. Did we stock out? When, for how long, revenue impact?
3. Was ad spend efficient? (ACoS vs. target, top/bottom 5 keywords by ROAS)
4. Did promotional pricing increase total profit? (volume gain vs. margin loss)
5. Return rate above 15%? Root cause?
6. What surprised us? (competitor moves, algorithm changes, behavior shifts)

**Output:** 1-page post-mortem per hero SKU + consolidated event report with
action items for next peak event.

## Chinese New Year Factory Closure Planning

CNY is the single most disruptive annual event for China-sourced sellers.
Total disruption window: 6-8 weeks (2-3 weeks slowdown + 2-4 weeks closure +
2-3 weeks ramp-up).

### CNY Key Actions

1. **Calculate cover:** `CNY Buffer = Avg Daily Sales x (Disruption Weeks x 7 + Ocean Days + FBA Receiving Days)`
2. **Place orders by October.** December orders risk missing pre-closure production.
3. **Ship in two batches:** 60% by mid-November, 40% by early December.
4. **Diversify critical SKUs** to non-CNY-affected suppliers as backup.
5. **Confirm actual last production day** with suppliers (workers leave 1-2 weeks early).

## Red Flags

### 1. Starting Preparation Less Than 60 Days Before a Peak Event

If you are building campaigns, ordering inventory, or optimizing listings with
fewer than 60 days until the event, you are already behind. For ocean-freight
sellers, 60 days is not even enough to receive a new inventory shipment. The
minimum viable planning window is 90 days; anything less means you are
reacting, not planning.

### 2. Allocating More Than 50% of Peak Budget to a Single Unproven SKU

Concentrating peak-season investment on a product that has not been validated
through at least 60 days of steady sales and 30+ reviews is a high-risk
gamble. If the product underperforms during the event, you cannot recover
the inventory cost. Spread risk across 3-5 proven SKUs and allocate no more
than 30% of total budget to any single product.

### 3. Ignoring Post-Peak Inventory Liquidation Timelines

Many sellers celebrate peak-season revenue without planning for the 20-40% of
inventory that does not sell through. Unsold peak-season inventory sitting in
FBA for 90+ days will incur long-term storage fees that erase the event's
profit. Set a hard deadline: if a SKU has more than 30 days of supply
remaining at T+14, activate the liquidation plan immediately.

### 4. Copying Last Year's Plan Without Adjustment

Markets, algorithms, competitors, and consumer behavior change year over year.
A keyword strategy that worked for Black Friday 2024 may underperform in 2025
if competitors have adopted the same terms or if the platform has changed its
ranking algorithm. Always rebuild the keyword list, re-analyze competitors,
and re-forecast demand using the most recent data.

### 5. Running Peak-Season Ads Without Prior Testing

Launching untested ad campaigns on Black Friday is equivalent to running an
experiment with your most expensive traffic. CPCs are 2-5x higher during peak
events, so inefficient campaigns burn budget rapidly. Every campaign should
have at least 14 days of pre-event data (from the T-30 warm-up phase) before
receiving peak-day budgets.
