# Supplier Sourcing

Find, vet, and negotiate with manufacturers and wholesalers so that every production
order is backed by verified samples and no single supplier controls your supply chain.

**Core Principle: Never place a production order with a supplier whose sample you have
not physically inspected. Never rely on a single supplier for more than 60% of any
SKU's volume.**

---

## The Sourcing Funnel

Sourcing is a systematic narrowing process. Rushing straight to a purchase order
is the most common and most expensive mistake new sellers make.

| Stage | Suppliers | Goal | Typical Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Search | 50+ | Cast a wide net across channels | 1-2 weeks |
| 2. Shortlist | 5-8 | Filter by vetting criteria | 3-5 days |
| 3. Sample | 3-4 | Order and physically inspect samples | 2-4 weeks |
| 4. Negotiate | 2 | Finalize price, MOQ, payment, lead time | 1-2 weeks |
| 5. PO | 1-2 | Place production order with backup supplier identified | Ongoing |

At every stage, document your findings in a supplier comparison spreadsheet. Columns
should include: supplier name, contact, channel found, MOQ, unit price at various
quantities, sample cost, lead time, payment terms, certifications, and notes.

## Channel Selection

Choose sourcing channels based on your product category, order volume, and risk
tolerance.

| Channel | Best For | Pros | Cons | Typical MOQ | Price Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1688.com** | Commodity products, small initial orders | Lowest prices, huge selection, factory-direct common | Chinese-only interface, domestic payment required, limited buyer protection | 2-100 units | Lowest |
| **Alibaba.com** | Mid-volume international orders | English interface, Trade Assurance escrow, RFQ system | Higher prices than 1688, many traders posing as factories | 100-500 units | Low-Medium |
| **Direct Factory** | Custom/branded products, large volume | Full control, best long-term pricing, IP protection | Requires on-site visits, high MOQ initially, relationship-heavy | 500-5000 units | Lowest (at volume) |
| **Canton Fair** | Diverse categories, building relationships | Meet suppliers face-to-face, see physical samples, compare many at once | Twice yearly only (Apr/Oct), travel cost, overwhelming scale | Varies | Medium |
| **Sourcing Agent** | Sellers without China presence, complex products | Handles communication/logistics/QC, local knowledge | Agent fee (3-10%), potential conflict of interest, less direct control | Varies | Medium-High (with fees) |

### Channel Selection Decision Tree

1. **Do you read Chinese and have Alipay?** Yes -> Start on 1688. No -> Start on Alibaba or use a sourcing agent.
2. **Is your target order volume > 1000 units?** Yes -> Invest in finding direct factories. No -> 1688 or Alibaba suppliers are fine initially.
3. **Is this a custom/branded product?** Yes -> Direct factory or agent recommended. No -> Marketplace sourcing is sufficient.
4. **Is this your first sourcing trip?** Yes -> Canton Fair is excellent for education and contacts. No -> Target specific factories.

## Supplier Vetting Criteria

Evaluate every shortlisted supplier against these seven criteria. Score each 1-5
and require a minimum total of 25/35 before proceeding to sample stage.

### 1. Years in Business (Weight: Medium)

- **5**: 10+ years, verifiable through business registration
- **4**: 5-9 years with stable operations
- **3**: 3-4 years
- **2**: 1-2 years
- **1**: Less than 1 year or unverifiable

**How to verify**: Request a copy of the business license (yingye zhizhao). Cross-check
the registration date on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System
(gsxt.gov.cn) or Tianyancha (tianyancha.com).

### 2. Factory vs. Trading Company (Weight: High)

- **5**: Verified factory with own production lines
- **4**: Factory with some outsourced processes
- **3**: Trading company with exclusive factory partnerships
- **2**: General trading company
- **1**: Unclear or evasive about status

**How to verify**: Ask for factory photos with timestamps. Request a video call tour
during working hours. Check if the business license scope says "manufacturing"
(shengchan) vs. "trade" (maoyi). On 1688, look for the "factory" (gongchang) badge.

### 3. Production Capacity (Weight: Medium)

- **5**: Can handle 5x your projected order volume
- **4**: Can handle 3-5x your volume
- **3**: Can handle 1-2x your volume comfortably
- **2**: Your order would be a significant portion of their capacity
- **1**: Capacity unclear or insufficient

**How to verify**: Ask for monthly output figures. Request photos of the factory floor
with machinery counts. Ask how many production lines they operate and what percentage
is currently committed.

### 4. Quality Certifications (Weight: High for regulated products)

- **5**: ISO 9001 + product-specific certifications (CE, FDA, ROHS) all current
- **4**: ISO 9001 + some product certifications
- **3**: Product-specific certifications only
- **2**: Basic certifications, some expired
- **1**: No certifications or unwilling to share

**How to verify**: Request certificate copies. Verify ISO certifications on the
issuing body's website. Check certificate numbers and expiry dates. Note that some
certificates can be purchased without real audits -- the issuing body reputation matters.

### 5. Communication Responsiveness (Weight: High)

- **5**: Responds within 4 hours during business hours, proactive updates
- **4**: Responds within 8 hours, answers questions completely
- **3**: Responds within 24 hours, sometimes needs follow-up
- **2**: Slow responses (48+ hours), incomplete answers
- **1**: Unresponsive or evasive

**How to assess**: Send initial inquiry with 5 specific questions. Time the response.
Evaluate completeness. Send a follow-up asking for clarification. This early
interaction predicts future communication quality.

### 6. Sample Quality (Weight: Highest)

- **5**: Sample meets or exceeds reference product quality
- **4**: Sample is acceptable with minor adjustments identified
- **3**: Sample has issues but supplier shows willingness to iterate
- **2**: Sample quality significantly below expectations
- **1**: Sample not provided or unusable

**How to assess**: Follow the Sample Order Protocol below. Compare against a reference
product you have purchased at retail. Document every defect with photos and measurements.

### 7. Payment Flexibility (Weight: Medium)

- **5**: Offers multiple payment options (T/T 30/70, Trade Assurance, LC)
- **4**: Standard 30/70 T/T with some flexibility
- **3**: Requires 50% deposit, balance before shipping
- **2**: Requires large deposit (70%+)
- **1**: Demands 100% upfront or cash only

**How to assess**: Ask directly about payment terms. A supplier confident in their
product and relationship will offer standard terms. Excessive upfront demands often
correlate with reliability issues.

## Sample Order Protocol

### Before Ordering

1. Request a product specification sheet from the supplier
2. Provide your exact requirements: dimensions, materials, colors, packaging, labeling
3. Agree on sample cost and shipping method (samples are rarely free; expect $20-200)
4. Confirm sample lead time (typically 5-15 days for existing products, 15-30 for custom)
5. Get a reference sample of the same or similar product from retail for comparison

### When Samples Arrive

Inspect every sample against this checklist:

- [ ] Dimensions match specification (measure with calipers, not estimates)
- [ ] Weight matches specification
- [ ] Material quality matches description
- [ ] Color accuracy (compare under natural and artificial light)
- [ ] Finish quality (no scratches, burrs, discoloration)
- [ ] Functional testing (operate the product 50+ times)
- [ ] Packaging integrity (simulate shipping damage)
- [ ] Labeling accuracy (barcodes scan correctly, text has no errors)
- [ ] Smell test (chemical odors indicate material or process issues)
- [ ] Drop test (drop packaged product from 1m onto hard surface, 6 faces)

### Documentation

- Photograph every sample from 6 angles plus detail shots of any defects
- Record measurements in a comparison table
- Video any functional tests
- Keep all samples labeled and stored for reference during production QC

### Decision

- All checks pass -> Proceed to negotiate production order
- Minor issues identified -> Send detailed feedback, request revised sample
- Major issues -> Discard supplier or request completely new sample with clear specs
- Never proceed to production with unresolved sample issues

## Red Flags in Suppliers

Watch for these warning signs. Any single red flag warrants caution; two or more
means walk away.

### 1. Ordering Production Without Testing Samples

**Why it is dangerous**: Production quality is almost always worse than sample quality.
If the sample is not acceptable, production will be worse. You will receive thousands
of unsellable units with no recourse.

**What to do instead**: Always order 2-3 samples. Test them thoroughly. Only then
negotiate production terms.

### 2. Single-Source Dependency

**Why it is dangerous**: Factory fires, government shutdowns, raw material shortages,
or simple quality deterioration can halt your entire business overnight. During COVID,
sellers with single suppliers lost months of sales.

**What to do instead**: Maintain at least two qualified suppliers for every SKU that
represents more than 20% of your revenue. Split orders 60/40 or 70/30. The slightly
higher cost is insurance.

### 3. Trusting "Gold Supplier" Badges as Quality Proof

**Why it is dangerous**: Gold Supplier, Verified Supplier, and similar badges on
Alibaba are paid memberships, not quality certifications. A supplier pays $2000-6000/year
for Gold Supplier status. It indicates marketing budget, not manufacturing capability.

**What to do instead**: Treat badges as a signal that the supplier is serious about
export business, nothing more. Apply the full 7-point vetting criteria regardless of
badges.

### 4. Paying 100% Upfront

**Why it is dangerous**: You lose all leverage once full payment is made. If quality
is poor, delivery is late, or specifications are wrong, you have no financial recourse.
Legitimate suppliers understand this and will not demand it.

**What to do instead**: Standard terms are 30% deposit, 70% after pre-shipment
inspection. For first orders with new suppliers, consider Trade Assurance or Letter
of Credit for additional protection.

### 5. Not Verifying Factory vs. Trading Company

**Why it is dangerous**: Trading companies add 10-30% markup and have no direct control
over production quality or timelines. When problems arise, they act as middlemen who
slow resolution. You may be paying premium prices for commodity-level supply chain
management.

**What to do instead**: Verify directly using business license checks. Ask for a live
video factory tour. If you determine the supplier is a trader, that is acceptable only
if they add real value (sourcing across categories, QC services, logistics coordination)
and you price accordingly.

## Quick Reference

### First-Time Sourcing Checklist

1. [ ] Define product specifications with measurements, materials, and reference photos
2. [ ] Search across at least two channels (e.g., 1688 + Alibaba)
3. [ ] Identify 50+ potential suppliers
4. [ ] Apply 7-point vetting to narrow to 5-8 shortlisted suppliers
5. [ ] Request samples from 3-4 suppliers
6. [ ] Complete sample inspection protocol for each
7. [ ] Negotiate with top 2 suppliers
8. [ ] Place initial production order with winner, keep runner-up warm
9. [ ] Arrange pre-shipment inspection before final payment
10. [ ] Document everything for future reorders

### Typical Timeline (First Order)

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Research and search | 1-2 weeks |
| Supplier vetting and shortlisting | 1 week |
| Sample ordering and shipping | 2-4 weeks |
| Sample evaluation | 3-5 days |
| Negotiation | 1-2 weeks |
| Production | 2-6 weeks (product dependent) |
| Pre-shipment inspection | 2-3 days |
| Shipping to destination | 1-6 weeks (air vs sea) |
| **Total** | **8-20 weeks** |

### Cost Components to Track

- Unit price (at negotiated volume)
- Sample cost
- Packaging cost (custom packaging adds $0.10-2.00/unit)
- Labeling and compliance marking cost
- Domestic shipping (factory to port/consolidator)
- Export customs and documentation fees
- International freight (air, sea, or express)
- Import duties and taxes
- Last-mile delivery to warehouse/FBA
