# Factory Audit and Sample Evaluation Guide

A systematic approach to verifying supplier manufacturing capability and evaluating
product samples before committing to production orders. This guide covers remote
audits (video call), on-site inspections, and ongoing quality assurance.

## 1. Pre-Audit Research

Before contacting the supplier for any audit, gather and verify background information
independently. This preparation takes 2-4 hours and prevents wasting time on
unqualified factories.

### Business License Verification

1. Request a copy of the supplier's business license (yingye zhizhao)
2. Key fields to extract from the license:
   - **Company name** (qiye mingcheng): Must match the name used in communications
   - **Unified social credit code** (tongyi shehui xinyong daima): The 18-digit
     unique business identifier
   - **Legal representative** (fading daibiaoren): The person legally responsible
   - **Registered capital** (zhuce ziben): Indicates company scale (though this can
     be inflated)
   - **Business scope** (jingying fanwei): Must include manufacturing (shengchan) or
     processing (jiagong) for your product type
   - **Registration date** (chengji riqi): Indicates how long the company has existed
   - **Operating period** (yingye qixian): Confirm it has not expired

3. Cross-reference on official verification platforms:
   - **National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System** (gsxt.gov.cn):
     Free, official government database. Search by company name or credit code.
   - **Tianyancha** (tianyancha.com): Commercial database with richer data including
     legal disputes, related companies, and patent filings.
   - **Qichacha** (qcc.com): Similar to Tianyancha with additional financial data.

4. Verify these details match:
   - Company name on the license matches the name on 1688/Alibaba profile
   - Business scope includes manufacturing for your product category
   - The company has not been flagged for abnormal operations (jingying yichang)
   - No major legal disputes listed (check the "judicial risks" tab on Tianyancha)

### Factory Location Analysis

1. Get the factory address from the business license
2. Verify the address on Baidu Maps (map.baidu.com) satellite view:
   - Does the satellite image show a factory building or an office building?
   - Is the location in an industrial zone or a commercial area?
   - What is the approximate size of the facility?
3. Check if the address is in a known manufacturing hub for your product category:
   - **Electronics**: Shenzhen, Dongguan, Huizhou
   - **Textiles/apparel**: Guangzhou, Shaoxing, Changshu
   - **Small commodities**: Yiwu
   - **Furniture**: Foshan
   - **Auto parts**: Wenzhou, Taizhou
   - **Toys**: Shantou, Dongguan
   - **Kitchenware**: Jieyang, Yangjiang
4. A factory that claims to manufacture electronics but is located in Yiwu (a trading
   hub) warrants extra scrutiny

### Financial Health Indicators

- Check annual revenue reports if available on Tianyancha/Qichacha
- Review any listed legal disputes (particularly unpaid supplier debts)
- Verify social insurance contributions (shebao) to estimate actual employee count
- Check for recent changes in legal representative (frequent changes indicate instability)

## 2. Remote Audit Checklist

A video call factory tour is the minimum verification step for any supplier you are
considering. It takes 30-60 minutes and eliminates most fraudulent or grossly
inadequate suppliers.

### Before the Call

- [ ] Schedule during factory working hours (typically 8:00-17:30 CST)
- [ ] Request the call be conducted from the factory floor, not an office or showroom
- [ ] Prepare your product specifications and reference images to discuss
- [ ] Test your video call platform (WeChat video is most common in China; WhatsApp
  or Zoom also work)
- [ ] Have a list of specific things you want to see (see below)

### During the Video Tour

#### Factory Environment (5 items)

- [ ] **Facility exterior**: Does it look like a real factory? Check for signage,
  loading docks, raw material storage
- [ ] **Factory floor size**: Ask the guide to walk the full floor; estimate square
  footage relative to claimed capacity
- [ ] **Cleanliness and organization**: A clean, organized factory generally produces
  better quality. Look for 5S methodology signs (sort, set, shine, standardize,
  sustain)
- [ ] **Worker count**: Roughly count workers visible. Compare against the supplier's
  claimed employee count
- [ ] **Safety equipment**: Workers should have appropriate PPE (masks, gloves, eye
  protection as needed for the product type)

#### Production Equipment (5 items)

- [ ] **Machine types**: Are the machines appropriate for your product? Ask the guide
  to zoom in on equipment nameplates to identify make/model
- [ ] **Machine age and condition**: Well-maintained older machines are fine; visibly
  damaged or neglected equipment is a red flag
- [ ] **Production lines**: How many lines are running? This indicates current capacity
  utilization
- [ ] **Automation level**: More automation generally means more consistent quality
  (but higher MOQ requirements)
- [ ] **Testing equipment**: Ask to see any in-line testing or QC equipment. Good
  factories test during production, not just at the end

#### Quality Control Area (4 items)

- [ ] **Dedicated QC area**: Separate space for quality inspection indicates a
  systematic approach
- [ ] **QC tools**: Calipers, gauges, testing equipment appropriate for the product
- [ ] **Defect samples**: Good QC areas have examples of common defects for worker
  reference
- [ ] **QC records**: Ask to see recent QC reports or inspection records (they can
  blur customer names)

#### Warehouse and Shipping (3 items)

- [ ] **Raw material storage**: Organized raw material storage indicates good supply
  chain management
- [ ] **Finished goods storage**: Proper packaging and labeling of finished inventory
- [ ] **Shipping area**: Evidence of regular shipments (stacked pallets, shipping
  labels, logistics partner signage)

### Red Flags During Remote Audit

- The supplier insists on showing only the showroom, not the factory floor
- The video quality is suspiciously poor (hiding details)
- The "factory" has only a few workers and minimal equipment but claims large capacity
- Equipment brand names are taped over or hidden
- The guide cannot answer basic questions about production processes
- Background noise does not match a factory environment
- The tour is obviously pre-rehearsed with specific camera angles only

## 3. On-Site Audit Checklist

An on-site visit is recommended for any supplier you plan to place orders exceeding
$10,000 or for any supplier who will produce branded/custom products. Budget one
full day per factory visit.

### Pre-Visit (3 items)

- [ ] Confirm visit date and time directly with factory management (not just sales)
- [ ] Arrange a translator if your Chinese is not business-fluent
- [ ] Prepare a printed copy of your product specifications and quality standards

### Facility and Environment (5 items)

- [ ] Factory matches the address on the business license
- [ ] Building is appropriate scale for claimed production capacity
- [ ] Factory is in an industrial zone (not residential or commercial)
- [ ] General cleanliness and organization of the entire facility
- [ ] Environmental compliance (waste disposal, emissions controls if applicable)

### Production Floor (8 items)

- [ ] Raw materials are properly stored and labeled
- [ ] Work-in-progress is organized and flows logically through stations
- [ ] Production equipment is well-maintained and appropriate for your product
- [ ] Workers demonstrate competence with their tasks (not obviously new or confused)
- [ ] Production line layout supports quality (inspection points between stages)
- [ ] Material handling is careful (no rough treatment of semi-finished products)
- [ ] Temperature and humidity are controlled if product requires it
- [ ] Dust and contamination controls are in place where needed

### Quality Management (7 items)

- [ ] Incoming material inspection (do they test raw materials before use?)
- [ ] In-process quality checkpoints (inspection between production stages)
- [ ] Final quality inspection area with documented procedures
- [ ] Measurement tools are calibrated (check calibration stickers for dates)
- [ ] Defect tracking system exists (records of defect types and rates)
- [ ] Non-conforming product is segregated and labeled (not mixed with good product)
- [ ] QC staff are separate from production staff (not self-inspecting)

### Documentation and Systems (5 items)

- [ ] Quality management system documentation is available (ISO or equivalent)
- [ ] Production records are maintained (batch tracking, material traceability)
- [ ] Customer complaint records exist and show follow-up actions
- [ ] Employee training records are maintained
- [ ] Supplier management system for their raw material suppliers

### Workforce and Social Compliance (4 items)

- [ ] Workers appear to be of legal working age
- [ ] Working conditions are safe (lighting, ventilation, fire exits, fire extinguishers)
- [ ] Workers are wearing appropriate PPE for their tasks
- [ ] No signs of excessive overtime (check time card systems if visible)

### Additional Inspection Points (3 items)

- [ ] Ask to see a current production run (not a showroom piece)
- [ ] Request to pull a random finished product from inventory for immediate inspection
- [ ] Check packaging materials storage (proper packaging prevents shipping damage)

### Post-Visit Documentation

- Photograph everything (with permission) -- factory exterior, equipment, production
  lines, QC area, warehousing
- Record the names and titles of people you met
- Note the actual worker count you observed vs. the claimed headcount
- Estimate the facility size in square meters
- Document any concerns or questions for follow-up
- Write up your audit findings within 24 hours while details are fresh

## 4. Quality Certifications Reference

Understanding which certifications matter for your product and target market.

### Management System Certifications

| Certification | What It Covers | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **ISO 9001** | Quality management system | Universal; indicates documented quality processes |
| **ISO 14001** | Environmental management | Important for eco-conscious markets; sometimes required by retailers |
| **ISO 45001** | Occupational health and safety | Indicates worker safety focus; relevant for social compliance |
| **BSCI** | Business Social Compliance Initiative | Required by many EU importers |
| **SA8000** | Social accountability | Comprehensive social compliance; valued by large retailers |

### Product Safety Certifications by Market

#### United States

| Certification | Applies To | Mandatory? |
|---|---|---|
| **FCC** | Electronic devices that emit RF energy | Yes, for applicable products |
| **UL** | Electrical safety (various UL standards) | Not legally mandatory but practically required by retailers and insurance |
| **CPSIA** | Children's products (lead, phthalates) | Yes, for children's products sold in the US |
| **FDA** | Food contact materials, cosmetics, medical devices | Yes, for applicable products |
| **ASTM F963** | Toy safety | Yes, for toys sold in the US |
| **EPA** | Pesticide-treated products, certain chemicals | Yes, for applicable products |

#### European Union

| Certification | Applies To | Mandatory? |
|---|---|---|
| **CE** | Broad range of products (safety, health, environment) | Yes, for applicable product categories |
| **REACH** | Chemical substance restrictions | Yes, for all products containing chemicals |
| **RoHS** | Restriction of hazardous substances in electronics | Yes, for electrical/electronic equipment |
| **EN 71** | Toy safety | Yes, for toys sold in the EU |
| **LFGB** | Food contact materials (Germany, widely adopted) | Yes in Germany, benchmark for EU |
| **WEEE** | Waste electrical and electronic equipment recycling | Yes, for electronics manufacturers |

#### Other Markets

| Certification | Market | Applies To |
|---|---|---|
| **PSE** | Japan | Electrical products |
| **JIS** | Japan | Industrial standards |
| **CCC** | China | Compulsory product certification |
| **KC** | South Korea | Safety certification |
| **SAA** | Australia | Electrical safety |
| **SASO** | Saudi Arabia | Product conformity |

### How to Verify Certifications

1. Request digital copies of all claimed certifications
2. Check certificate expiry dates (expired certificates are worthless)
3. Verify the certificate number with the issuing body:
   - ISO: Check with the specific certification body listed on the certificate
   - UL: ul.com/database (search by file number)
   - CE: No central database, but verify the Notified Body number if applicable
   - FCC: fcc.gov/oet/ea/fccid (search by FCC ID)
4. Confirm the certificate covers the specific product you are sourcing (not just the
   company in general)
5. Verify the certified company name matches your supplier's legal name

## 5. Sample Evaluation Protocol

### Preparation

Before evaluating samples, establish your quality baseline:

1. **Reference product**: Purchase the same or similar product at retail in your
   target market. This is your quality benchmark.
2. **Specification sheet**: Document every measurable attribute of the product you
   want (dimensions, weight, material, color, finish, functionality).
3. **Accept/reject criteria**: Define what passes and what fails before you see the
   samples. This prevents bias.
4. **Testing equipment**: Prepare calipers, a precision scale, a color reference card,
   and any product-specific testing tools.

### Physical Inspection Checklist

#### Dimensions and Weight

- [ ] Measure length, width, height with calipers (not ruler)
- [ ] Record weight to the nearest gram
- [ ] Compare all measurements to specification sheet
- [ ] Define acceptable tolerance (typically +/- 2% for dimensions, +/- 5% for weight)
- [ ] Measure 100% of samples -- variation between identical samples indicates
  inconsistent manufacturing

#### Materials and Construction

- [ ] Material matches specification (touch test, flex test, scratch test)
- [ ] No sharp edges or burrs that could injure users
- [ ] Seams, joints, and fasteners are secure
- [ ] No loose parts or components
- [ ] Assembly (if applicable) fits together without forcing
- [ ] Moving parts operate smoothly
- [ ] Surface finish is consistent (no pitting, bubbles, rough patches)

#### Visual Quality

- [ ] Color matches specification (compare under both natural daylight and indoor
  LED lighting)
- [ ] No visible defects (scratches, dents, stains, discoloration)
- [ ] Printing/labeling is clear and correctly positioned
- [ ] Branding elements (if custom) are accurate
- [ ] Overall aesthetic impression matches or exceeds reference product

#### Functional Testing

- [ ] Product performs its primary function correctly
- [ ] Operate the product 50+ times to test durability under normal use
- [ ] Test all features and modes
- [ ] Test with accessories/components it will be used with
- [ ] For electronic products: power on/off cycling 20+ times
- [ ] For products with lids/closures: open/close cycling 100+ times

#### Packaging Evaluation

- [ ] Inner packaging protects product adequately
- [ ] Packaging materials are appropriate (no excess, no insufficient)
- [ ] Barcodes scan correctly with a barcode scanner app
- [ ] Product fits in packaging without excessive space or compression
- [ ] Unboxing experience is acceptable for your target market
- [ ] Packaging text is accurate and free of errors

#### Stress Testing

- [ ] Drop test: Drop packaged product from 1 meter onto hard surface, each face
- [ ] Compression test: Stack weight on packaged product to simulate shipping
- [ ] Temperature test: Leave product in direct sunlight for 4 hours and in a
  refrigerator for 4 hours (if relevant to product type)
- [ ] Water resistance test: If product claims any water resistance
- [ ] Load test: Apply expected loads plus 50% safety margin

### Documentation

For every sample evaluated:

1. **Photo documentation**: 6 standard angles (front, back, left, right, top, bottom)
   plus detail shots of any defects, labels, packaging, and any notable features
2. **Measurement log**: Table of all dimensional and weight measurements vs.
   specification
3. **Test results log**: Pass/fail for each functional and stress test
4. **Comparison notes**: How this sample compares to the reference product and to
   samples from other suppliers
5. **Overall verdict**: Accept / Accept with conditions (list conditions) / Reject
   (list reasons)

## 6. First Production Run Quality Check

The first production run (also called the pilot run or initial production batch) is
the highest-risk order. Quality from this run sets the standard for all future orders.

### Pre-Production Confirmation

Before production starts, confirm in writing:

- [ ] Final product specification is signed off by both parties
- [ ] Approved sample is held by both you and the supplier as the reference standard
- [ ] Packaging specifications are confirmed (artwork, materials, dimensions)
- [ ] Labeling and compliance markings are approved
- [ ] Production quantity and delivery schedule are confirmed
- [ ] Quality acceptance criteria are documented (AQL levels, critical/major/minor
  defect definitions)

### During Production Inspection (DPI)

Schedule an inspection when approximately 20-30% of the order is complete:

- [ ] Production is following the agreed specification
- [ ] Materials being used match the approved sample
- [ ] In-line quality checks are happening
- [ ] Any issues identified can be corrected before full production completes
- [ ] Random pull of completed units for spot-check inspection

### Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI)

This is the most critical inspection. Conduct when 100% of goods are produced
and at least 80% are packed.

#### Sampling Plan (AQL-Based)

Use the ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 standard (also called ISO 2859-1) Acceptable Quality Limit
sampling plan:

| Order Size | Sample Size (General Level II) | Accept/Reject (AQL 2.5) |
|---|---|---|
| 2-8 | 2 | Ac: 0 / Re: 1 |
| 9-15 | 3 | Ac: 0 / Re: 1 |
| 16-25 | 5 | Ac: 0 / Re: 1 |
| 26-50 | 8 | Ac: 0 / Re: 1 |
| 51-90 | 13 | Ac: 1 / Re: 2 |
| 91-150 | 20 | Ac: 1 / Re: 2 |
| 151-280 | 32 | Ac: 2 / Re: 3 |
| 281-500 | 50 | Ac: 3 / Re: 4 |
| 501-1200 | 80 | Ac: 5 / Re: 6 |
| 1201-3200 | 125 | Ac: 7 / Re: 8 |
| 3201-10000 | 200 | Ac: 10 / Re: 11 |

**AQL level guidance:**
- **Critical defects** (safety hazards): AQL 0 -- zero tolerance
- **Major defects** (product does not function as intended): AQL 2.5
- **Minor defects** (cosmetic imperfections): AQL 4.0

#### PSI Inspection Items

- [ ] Pull samples according to AQL sampling plan
- [ ] Verify product matches approved sample (side-by-side comparison)
- [ ] Check all dimensions against specification
- [ ] Conduct functional testing on all pulled samples
- [ ] Inspect packaging and labeling for accuracy
- [ ] Verify carton markings (shipping marks, quantity labels, handling symbols)
- [ ] Check carton weight and dimensions against packing list
- [ ] Conduct a drop test on one master carton from each packing configuration
- [ ] Count total quantity against purchase order
- [ ] Photograph representative samples and any defects found

### Inspection Decision

- **Pass**: Defects are within AQL limits. Authorize final payment and shipment.
- **Conditional pass**: Minor issues found. Supplier must fix or sort before shipment.
  Re-inspect the corrected portion.
- **Fail**: Defects exceed AQL limits. Do not authorize payment. Negotiate with the
  supplier: rework, replacement, price reduction, or order cancellation.

## 7. Ongoing Quality Assurance Framework

Quality assurance does not end after the first order. Establish a system for
continuous monitoring.

### Order-to-Order Quality Tracking

Maintain a quality scorecard for each supplier across orders:

| Metric | Target | How to Track |
|---|---|---|
| Defect rate (critical) | 0% | PSI inspection results |
| Defect rate (major) | < 2.5% | PSI inspection results |
| Defect rate (minor) | < 4.0% | PSI inspection results |
| On-time delivery | > 95% | Actual vs. promised ship date |
| Specification compliance | 100% | Dimensional checks per shipment |
| Customer complaint rate | < 1% | Post-sale return/complaint data |
| Communication responsiveness | < 8 hours | Average response time tracking |

### Escalation Triggers

Take action when any of these thresholds are crossed:

- **Warning**: 2 consecutive orders with major defect rate > 2%
- **Corrective action required**: Any critical defect found in production
- **Supplier review**: Customer complaint rate exceeds 2% for 2 consecutive months
- **Supplier replacement**: 3 consecutive orders failing PSI, or any single order
  with critical safety defects

### Corrective Action Process

1. Document the quality issue with photos, measurements, and affected quantities
2. Send a formal corrective action request (CAR) to the supplier
3. The CAR must include: problem description, root cause analysis request, corrective
   action plan, preventive measures, and implementation timeline
4. Supplier must respond within 5 business days with their root cause analysis and
   proposed corrective actions
5. Verify corrective actions are implemented in the next production run
6. If corrective actions are ineffective after 2 attempts, begin qualifying a
   replacement supplier

### Scheduled Re-Audits

- Conduct a full audit (remote or on-site) annually for active suppliers
- Conduct additional audits when: production volume increases significantly,
  new product lines are added, quality issues are detected, or ownership/management
  changes occur
- Compare re-audit findings against previous audit to verify improvement
